Who he is: a facet-level study of a very high-N, very low-E, very high-O profile

This document reads a single NEO-PI-R protocol — 240 self-report items, five domain scales and thirty facet scales, plus the printed validity block and the printed T-score profile — and turns it into a description of a person: how he thinks, what he does on an ordinary Tuesday, what he avoids, what he is good at, where he breaks, whom he should work for, whom he should marry, and which MBTI type best matches the shape of the scores.

It is long on purpose. A five-line summary of this profile is easy ("introverted, anxious, unconventional, honest, dutiful but disorganised") and almost useless, because none of those adjectives tells you what happens when a man with this particular combination is put in a meeting, a marriage or a crisis. The value is in the interactions between scales, so most of what follows works at the level of pairs and triples of facets rather than single scores.

What this is and is not. This is an interpretive reading of a self-report personality inventory, using the standard NEO-PI-R facet model (Costa & McCrae). It is not a diagnosis, not a clinical assessment, and not a prediction of any specific act. Personality scores describe dispositions and typical tendencies — probability shifts, not destinies. Everything below is a hypothesis with a stated basis in the numbers; where the numbers are thin or contradictory, that is said out loud. The subject answered the questionnaire himself; his self-view and his behaviour can diverge, and a few sections address exactly where that is likely.

1. How to read this protocol

1.1 The instrument

The NEO-PI-R measures the five broad domains of the Five-Factor Model — Neuroticism (N), Extraversion (E), Openness to Experience (O), Agreeableness (A) and Conscientiousness (C) — and splits each domain into six facets, thirty in total. Each facet is scored from eight items on a five-point Likert scale, which is why the facet scores on this sheet sit between roughly 4 and 30, and why the domain scores sit between roughly 50 and 135: each domain is the sum of its six facets.

That arithmetic is worth checking, because it tells us the sheets are internally consistent. On this protocol:

1.2 Two scoring keys, two different stories

The sheet carries two interpretation keys. The main table calls A 121 "خیلی بالا" (very high) and C 103 "متوسط" (average). The block labelled نظریه ("theory") calls the same two numbers "متوسط" and "پایین". Same answers, different norms or a different display convention. That is a real feature of this protocol, not a transcription error, and it is the first thing an honest reader has to handle.

My resolution: treat raw facet scores as the ground truth, use the printed labels only as a rough cross-check, and let the facet pattern decide the domain story. When A is examined facet by facet, it is not "very high" in the ordinary sense — it is high in candour and modesty and low in trust, altruism, compliance and tender-mindedness. That mixture produces a domain total near the middle of the range, exactly as the "theory" key says. The main key's "very high" appears to be computed on a different norm table (plausibly a local male norm where the raw distribution sits lower). Both can be true of the same 121; only one of them describes him.

The same caution applies to the labels in the facet columns. Several are clearly computed on a coarse or local norm: N1 = 21 out of 32 (66% of the maximum) is labelled "very high", while E1 = 12 (37.5%) is labelled "very low". A 66% score being "very high" and a 37% score being "very low" implies the interpretive bands are not equally spaced around the midpoint. So the labels are used here as ordinal signals, and the percentages are used to place a facet on the scale. Relative ranking within his own profile — which facet is his highest, which his lowest — is the most reliable signal of all, because it needs no external norm.

Signal 1 — the E floor. A domain at 28.6% of maximum, with two facets at 12.5%, is not "somewhat introverted". Practically: any plan that requires him to be socially available, socially assertive or socially energetic for hours will fail, and it will fail quietly — he will agree and then not show up, or show up and be blank.

Signal 2 — the honesty cluster. A2 at 27 with A5 at 23 and O6 at 28 forms a coherent triad: he tells the truth, he does not inflate himself, and he is willing to question consensus. Signal 3 — the duty/disorder split inside Conscientiousness. C3 at 29 and C1 at 25 against C2 at 9, C4 at 10, C5 at 11. He is principled and unreliable about logistics, which is a very specific and very recognisable kind of colleague.

Signal 4 — the openness split. O5, O6 and O2 at 26–28 against O4 at 9. His openness is entirely in the mind and the eye: ideas, values, beauty. It is not in the legs. He will read three books about a country and never go there. This distinction matters for career advice (he should be paid to think, not to travel) and for relationships (he is stimulating in conversation and inert on Saturday).

He is honest and humble but not trusting, not generous by default, not accommodating and not soft. His cooperation is principled (he will help because it is right, or because you asked properly) rather than affiliative (he will not help because his heart melts). People who need warmth to feel helped will find him cold; people who need reliability will find him solid.

One-sentence version of the foundation. Take a man with a scholar's mind, a monk's social appetite, a clerk's sense of duty and a sceptic's tongue, add a continuous low hum of anxiety and a complete inability to keep a filing system, and you have the entire protocol in a sentence.

4. Domain by domain

4.1 Neuroticism — 120 / 192 (62.5%) — a turbulent interior

Neuroticism is the disposition to experience negative affect: fear, sadness, embarrassment, anger, guilt, disgust. It is not a measure of mental illness; it is a measure of how easily and how often the threat-and-loss system fires. At 62.5% of the scale maximum with five of six facets in the upper half, this man's system fires readily and stays on for a while.

What is high. N1 Anxiety (21) — free-floating worry, anticipation of bad outcomes, tension in the body that has no obvious object. N4 Self-consciousness (23) — the sensation of being visible, evaluated, and probably found wanting; the hot-faced awareness of one's own awkwardness. N3 Depression (20) — not necessarily clinical depression, but a proneness to low mood, self-blame, discouragement and difficulty shaking off sadness. N5 Impulsiveness (20) — difficulty resisting urges, especially when the urge promises relief from tension. N6 Vulnerability (20) — the sense of being unable to cope when demands pile up, a low threshold for overwhelm.

What is comparatively lower. N2 Angry hostility (16) — the irritability facet. Still above the midpoint of the scale, but the lowest of his six. This single fact reshapes the whole N story: his distress turns inward. He does not shout at people; he goes quiet, he re-reads the same paragraph four times, he lies awake constructing arguments he will never deliver.

The internal architecture. Notice the loop this creates. Anxiety (N1) produces anticipatory worry about a situation. Self-consciousness (N4) makes him avoid the situation rather than test it. Avoidance prevents disconfirming evidence, so the worry survives. Depression (N3) supplies the interpretation "and anyway I'm not good at this". Vulnerability (N6) then converts a normal workload into a feeling of being swamped, and Impulsiveness (N5) provides the escape hatch: an unplanned purchase, a sudden decision, a night of scrolling, a cigarette, a late-night message he will regret. That loop — worry, avoid, ruminate, escape — is the single most important dynamic in his psychology and it is entirely self-contained. Nothing in it requires another person. That is why it persists: no one else ever sees it well enough to interrupt it.

What it looks like from outside. Very little. High-N internalisers are masters of the flat functional face. Colleagues see a quiet, competent man who sometimes seems distant. They do not see the pre-meeting dread, the replay of a sentence he said two days ago, the 2 a.m. inventory of everything that could go wrong with the project. The cost is invisible and therefore uncompensated — nobody adjusts a workload for a person who never complains, and nobody offers reassurance to a person who never asks.

The useful side. This is not a defect to be deleted, and it cannot be. High N buys three things: early warning (he notices problems before others, because he is constantly scanning for them), thoroughness under threat (anxious people prepare), and ethical vigilance (guilt-proneness and dutifulness compound into someone who will not cut corners quietly). In his case, N combined with C3 at 29 and A2 at 27 produces a person whose anxiety is expressed as conscientious honesty, not as drama. That is a rare and employable configuration. The goal is never to make him low-N; the goal is to keep the loop from closing.

The specific risks. (1) Somatic load — chronic tension, sleep-onset problems, digestive complaints, headaches, all medically unremarkable and psychologically informative. (2) Depressive episodes during low-structure periods, especially unemployment, long holidays, retirement, or a project ending. (3) Impulsive relief-seeking at exactly the moments when he is most depleted, which is when the consequences are worst. (4) Isolation as a coping strategy that then feeds N3. (5) Decision paralysis on irreversible choices (marriage, children, emigration, quitting), because every branch is examined for its failure mode.

What actually helps, specifically for this profile. Not "relax more" — useless advice for a scanning mind. The effective interventions for high-N internalisers are structure that removes choice (fixed sleep time, fixed exercise slot, pre-committed decisions), externalised worry (writing the fear down converts an ambient hum into a bounded object), behavioural experiments (one small disconfirmed fear per week, logged), and short-horizon goals (a week, not a year — long horizons feed N1 and N3). Social support helps, but he will not seek it, so it has to be scheduled rather than hoped for.

4.2 Extraversion — 55 / 192 (28.6%) — the smallest battery in the building

Extraversion is the disposition toward social attention, positive affect, energy and stimulation. It is not sociability alone; it is the whole reward system around people, novelty, pace and buzz. At 28.6% of maximum, with five of six facets in the bottom third, this is one of the lowest Extraversion profiles a NEO-PI-R produces.

The floor. E2 Gregariousness = 4. E3 Assertiveness = 4. These are not typos. On sixteen items about preferring company and taking charge, he endorsed almost nothing. In practical terms: he does not want to be in the room, and if he is in the room he does not want to be the one talking.

The rest of the block. E4 Activity = 7 — low tempo, low physical restlessness, no urge to fill time with doing. E1 Warmth = 12 — he does not radiate friendliness; his default face is neutral and his default tone is dry. E5 Excitement seeking = 13 — no attraction to thrills, crowds, gambling, fast cars, loud places. E6 Positive emotions = 15 — the highest facet in the domain, which matters: he is not anhedonic. He can feel delight. He simply feels it quietly and mostly alone, usually in response to an idea, a piece of music or a well-made thing.

The crucial distinction: low E is not social anxiety. Those are different constructs and this profile contains both, which is why they get confused. Social anxiety is N4 (self-consciousness) plus avoidance: fear of judgment. Low E is a preference: the absence of reward from social contact. He has both, so his behaviour is doubly withdrawn, but the two need different treatment. The low E will not change and does not need to. The N4-driven avoidance is what causes suffering and is what responds to exposure work.

Energy accounting. Think of his social capacity as a small tank with a slow pump. A two-hour dinner with four people costs most of it. A full day of meetings costs two days. A wedding costs a week. This is not metaphor; it is the practical arithmetic by which he should plan his life, and the arithmetic that others consistently fail to believe because he does not look tired, he looks bored, and boredom is read as rudeness.

What he is good at because of it. Sustained solitary concentration is a genuine competitive advantage in knowledge work. He can sit with a problem for six hours; most people cannot sit with it for one. He is not distracted by the social rewards that pull extroverts off-task (the meeting after the meeting, the visibility project, the networking lunch). He forms few but unusually durable attachments. And because he does not need an audience, his work does not degrade when nobody is watching — a rarer trait than it sounds.

What it costs him. Visibility. In almost every organisation, the person who speaks in the meeting is the person who gets remembered, and the person who gets remembered gets the promotion. With E3 at 4, he will lose that competition systematically, regardless of merit. Second cost: the social capital that protects people during reorganisations, disputes and blame events. Third: dating and friendship formation, both of which reward initiation, and initiation is precisely what he does not do.

Compensations that actually work for a low-E person. (1) Written channels — he is far better in text than in speech, and text is asynchronous, which suits both his tempo and his precision. (2) Small-group, task-focused contact — two people, a shared problem, a fixed end time. (3) One recurring social anchor per week, chosen by someone else, at a fixed time. (4) A single "sponsor" at work: one extroverted ally who transmits his work to the room. (5) Deliberate recovery blocks scheduled after any social obligation, so the cost is budgeted rather than borrowed.

4.3 Openness — 133 / 192 (69.3%) — a rich interior, a cautious exterior

Openness is the disposition toward imagination, aesthetic experience, emotional depth, novelty of action, intellectual curiosity and non-dogmatic values. It is the domain most strongly associated with creativity, with liberal and questioning attitudes, and with the ability to hold a hypothetical in mind. At 69.3% with two facets at 87.5%, this is high — but it is high in a very specific pattern.

The high facets. O5 Ideas = 28. This is intellectual appetite as a basic need: he reads for pleasure, collects frameworks, relishes a well-formed argument, and finds a good conversation about a bad book more satisfying than a good meal. O6 Values = 28. This is readiness to re-examine received truths; low scores mean "the old ways are right because they are ours", high scores mean "show me the evidence and I'll change my mind". At 28, he is close to the ceiling: no tradition, authority or consensus is automatically credible to him. O2 Aesthetics = 26 — a strong, involuntary, almost physical response to beauty, proportion, sound, wording. He will notice the typography on a form. O3 Feelings = 23 — his own emotional states are interesting and legible to him; he can name what he feels and is not embarrassed by complexity.

The one low facet, and why it matters. O4 Actions = 9 (28%). This is the "openness to experience" that involves the body and the calendar: trying a new cuisine, taking an unfamiliar route, moving country, changing routine, experimenting with lifestyle. At 28% he actively prefers the known procedure. His favourite restaurant is the one he always goes to; his route is the route; his weekend is the weekend.

O4 at 9 alongside O5 and O6 at 28 produces one of the most recognisable configurations in the whole model: the armchair radical. His novelty hunger is entirely conceptual. He will enthusiastically dismantle a received idea in an evening and then eat the same dinner for the ninth time. He will argue for radical change in the abstract and feel uneasy when the change arrives at his own door. He is open-minded about everything except his habits.

O1 = 19, the ambiguous facet. Transcribed as "تحمل" on the sheet (in the standard NEO-PI-R this facet is fantasy/imagination). At 59% it sits mid-range: some capacity for imaginative escape, daydreaming, hypothetical scenarios, not the full fantasy life of the novelist and not the earthbound literalism of the low scorer. Combined with high O5, the realistic reading is: imagination is instrumental for him — he imagines in order to solve or to argue, less often for its own sake.

What the O pattern predicts. (1) A wide, unsystematic reading life: many topics, deep in some, shallow in others, no syllabus. (2) Strong opinions about taste — music, film, prose style, design, food — held with unusual confidence and expressed bluntly (O2 + A2). (3) Discomfort with dogma in any form: religious, political, managerial, familial. He will not be a joiner. (4) Boredom as his most aversive state — far worse than loneliness. A boring job is a physical punishment for this profile; a lonely one is tolerable. (5) Aesthetic sensitivity that doubles as a vulnerability: ugly environments, ugly writing, ugly behaviour genuinely distress him and he has little tolerance for "good enough".

The trap. High O plus low C4 and low E3 produces the classic "under-realised talent" pattern: capable of original work, no engine to push it out, no appetite to sell it, no patience for the unglamorous middle of any project. Ideas arrive easily and finish rarely. He probably has notebooks, files, half-built projects and a private conviction that he could do something substantial if conditions were right. They will not become right on their own.

A6 = 18, low tender-mindedness. This facet measures how much one is moved by others' suffering and by sentimentality. Low scorers are not cruel; they are unsentimental. They respond to hardship with practical analysis rather than emotional mirroring. If you cry to him, he will offer a solution. If a policy harms a group, he will ask whether the numbers justify the policy. People who need emotional attunement before practical help will feel unseen by him; people who want the problem solved will find him excellent.

A5 = 23, the modesty. This is the redeeming facet. Modesty with candour produces a very particular social style: he will tell you your work is weak and will not tell you his is strong. He does not compete for credit, does not name-drop, does not angle for status. This makes his bluntness tolerable to most people, because it is visibly not self-serving. It is the reason he keeps relationships despite the abrasiveness.

The composite. He is a man of principle and poor diplomacy. He cooperates when cooperation is right, not when it is pleasant. He is loyal in the sense of keeping commitments, not in the sense of taking sides. His affection is real but expressed as reliability and truth, not as warmth and mirroring. This is a perfectly workable personality in the right culture and a miserable one in a culture that runs on charm.

The defining split. C3 at 29 and C2 at 9 are twenty points apart. This is not a mildly disorganised conscientious person; it is two different systems in one domain. Duty is intact and heroic — if he has committed to something, he will deliver it, including at cost to himself. Administration is absent — forms go unfiled, receipts vanish, deadlines are remembered but appointments are missed, and the desk is an archaeological site.

Why C3 = 29 is the highest score in the profile. Dutifulness is the facet closest to moral identity: keeping promises, honouring obligations, doing what is expected of a role. At 91% it is the load-bearing wall of his character. It is also, in combination with high N and low E, the reason he stays in situations that make him unhappy: leaving would be a failure of duty, and duty outranks wellbeing in his internal hierarchy. Expect him to remain years too long in a job, a city, or a relationship that has stopped working.

C4 = 10 and C5 = 11: the missing engine. Achievement striving is the appetite for accomplishment, for beating standards and for advancement. Self-discipline is the ability to begin and persist on unglamorous tasks. With both near the bottom third, he has capability without propulsion. He performs when the task is intrinsically interesting (O5), when a deadline is externally imposed, or when a commitment has been made (C3). He does not perform out of ambition, and he does not perform out of habit. This predicts a career that under-uses his ability, and a recurring private frustration about it.

C6 = 19 with N5 = 20: the contradiction worth flagging. Deliberation measures careful thinking before acting; impulsiveness measures the tendency to act on the urge of the moment. At mid-scale deliberation and high impulsiveness, the pattern is not "careful man who sometimes slips". It is two modes: long, slow, over-analysed deliberation on anything that matters, and sudden unconsidered action on anything that promises relief. He will research a laptop for three weeks and then buy shoes at midnight. He will spend a year not deciding to change jobs and then resign in a single afternoon.

What the C profile means for work. He is the person you want on the deliverable and not the person you want on the process. Quality of output: high. Reliability of commitment: very high. Reliability of administration: low. Give him a defined problem with a real deadline and an honest standard and he will produce work above his pay grade. Give him an open-ended role with self-managed scheduling and he will drift, then panic, then deliver something brilliant at 3 a.m. two days late.

What it means for life. Chronic low order plus high duty produces a specific kind of domestic chaos: unpaid bills that are eventually paid with a penalty, documents that exist but cannot be found, a home that is clean in patches and chaotic in others, and a private, nagging sense of being an adult who has not got the admin of life under control. He probably knows this and feels it as a small permanent shame, which the N3 facet converts into self-criticism.

5. The thirty facets, dossier by dossier

Each dossier follows the same structure: what the facet actually measures, his score and its placement, what it looks like in daily behaviour, how it interacts with the rest of his profile, the specific risk, and the specific practice that has some evidence behind it. Nothing here is generic advice; each recommendation is written for this score pattern.

5.1 Neuroticism facets

N1 Anxiety — 21 / 32 (66%) · printed label: very high

Measures: proneness to worry, tension, apprehension, and the anticipation of threat. High scorers are vigilant, uneasy, and often physically tense; low scorers are calm and slow to fear.

His pattern: At 21 with the printed label "very high", this is one of his load-bearing traits. The worry is not about one thing; it is a background process. It selects objects — the meeting, the health symptom, the email not yet answered, the money, the parents' health — but the process runs whether or not an object is available.

In daily behaviour: Arrives early to things that could go wrong. Rehearses conversations. Checks that the door is locked, then checks again. Reads a message twice before replying, and constructs a reply, deletes it, and writes a shorter one. Has difficulty falling asleep on Sunday nights. Notices the one sentence in a performance review that was critical and forgets the nine that were positive. Carries a low-grade sense that something is about to require him.

Interactions: With E3 at 4, the anxiety is never expressed as a demand for reassurance — he will not ask "was that okay?", he will simply worry privately, which means nobody ever calibrates him. With C3 at 29, anxiety converts into preparedness rather than avoidance in the domains where duty is involved, which is why he is reliable. With O5 at 28, the worry has material to work with: a mind that knows many things can construct many failure scenarios, and his scenarios are unusually well-informed and therefore unusually convincing.

Risk: Generalised worry that consumes cognitive bandwidth without producing action; sleep disruption; a slow drift toward avoidance of anything ambiguous.

Practice: A fixed "worry window" — fifteen minutes, same time daily, on paper, with the rule that worries outside the window get written down and deferred. This is standard stimulus-control for rumination and it suits a mind that needs its concerns acknowledged rather than suppressed. Alongside it: a scheduled 20-minute daily walk, because anxiety is partly a metabolic state and walking metabolises it more reliably than thinking does.

N2 Angry hostility — 16 / 32 (50%) · printed label: high

Measures: irritability, resentment, the readiness to feel anger and to express it. High scorers are easily provoked and often feel treated unfairly; low scorers are slow to anger and rarely resentful.

His pattern: 16 is at the exact midpoint of the scale and it is the lowest of his six N facets. This is important diagnostic information: his distress is not rage-shaped. He gets irritated — by incompetence, by noise, by vapid conversation, by people who waste his time — but the irritation does not organise into hostility, and he does not seek confrontation.

In daily behaviour: A visible micro-expression of contempt when someone says something glib. Sarcasm, dry and brief. Avoidance of the person rather than an argument with the person. Internal monologue that is considerably sharper than anything he says out loud. Occasional blunt remark that lands harder than he intended, because it carries an irritation he had not admitted to.

Interactions: With A4 at 14 (low compliance), the irritation is not converted into compliance — he will not swallow and smile. With E3 at 4, it is not converted into confrontation either. The result is a third path: silent non-cooperation. He stops contributing, answers in monosyllables, and does exactly what was asked and no more. With A2 at 27, the occasional sentence that escapes is completely truthful and completely undiplomatic, which is how a man who rarely gets angry acquires a reputation for being sharp-tongued.

Risk: Passive resistance that others read as sulking or as an attitude problem; resentment accumulating invisibly until a relationship ends abruptly for reasons the other person never saw coming.

Practice: Name the irritation out loud at the moment it is small — one sentence, no accusation, no heat ("when the deadline moved twice I got frustrated"). This is not conflict; it is information, and for a man with A2 at 27 he is already equipped to say true things. The skill to add is timing: say it while it is trivial.

N3 Depression — 20 / 32 (63%) · printed label: high

Measures: proneness to low mood, sadness, guilt, self-blame, discouragement and a reduced capacity for pleasure. This is a temperament measure, not a clinical diagnosis.

His pattern: 20 means he knows this state well. The mood is cyclical rather than constant — periods of flatness and self-criticism that arrive without an external cause and leave the same way. During these periods his already-low activity drops further, and his already-low social contact goes to zero.

In daily behaviour: Whole weekends that pass in a single position. A tendency to re-read old failures as evidence of a pattern. Difficulty answering "what did you do this week?" because nothing feels like it counts. Loss of interest in things he genuinely loves, which is the most alarming signal for him because his interests are the most reliable part of his life. Self-critical comparison with peers who are married, promoted, published, or simply more visibly alive.

Interactions: This is the facet that interacts most dangerously with the rest. Low E means he will not reach out. Low A3 means he will not be reached by obligation. Low C5 means he will not maintain the routines that protect mood. High O3 means he feels the state vividly rather than numbing it. High O5 means he can construct an intellectual justification for the mood ("this is just what my life is"). The combination produces a man who understands his depression better than his doctor and does less about it than a stranger would.

Risk: Episodes lengthening and deepening, particularly in low-structure periods (unemployment, holidays, the end of a project, retirement); alcohol or sleep used as anaesthesia; the intellectualised version, where insight substitutes for treatment.

Practice: Two hard rules instead of inspiration. (1) Behavioural activation: one scheduled activity with another person per week, arranged in advance, non-negotiable, regardless of mood. (2) A written early-warning list of his own first three signs, shared with one person who is allowed to say "you're in it" — because his judgment during an episode is exactly the faculty that is impaired.

N4 Self-consciousness — 23 / 32 (72%) · printed label: very high

Measures: sensitivity to evaluation, embarrassment, the feeling of being observed, social insecurity and shame-proneness. It is the social face of neuroticism.

His pattern: At 72% this is his highest neuroticism facet and his seventh-highest score overall. The experience is not "I am bad at socialising" (though he would endorse that); it is "I am visible and I am being assessed". Any situation with an audience — a meeting where he might be asked something, a family gathering, a phone call, a photo, a queue — carries a small tax.

In daily behaviour: Sits in the seat with the least exposure. Speaks last or not at all. Replays a sentence he said at 11 a.m. at 11 p.m. Avoids phone calls in favour of text, and delays calls for days. Dislikes being photographed. Does not ask for help, because asking exposes not-knowing. Over-prepares for anything public and then performs below his preparation, because arousal interferes with retrieval.

Interactions: N4 plus E3 at 4 is the exact combination that produces the "invisible expert": the person who knows the answer, does not raise a hand, and is quietly furious when someone else says it worse. N4 plus A5 at 23 (modesty) means he does not correct the record when he is underestimated. N4 plus O5 at 28 means he has the vocabulary to describe his own avoidance with painful accuracy, which he mistakes for having addressed it.

Risk: Career stagnation by non-participation; loneliness maintained by refusal to initiate; a self-image built almost entirely from imagined external judgments rather than actual feedback.

Practice: Exposure in graded steps with a written prediction before each step, because the mechanism that maintains N4 is the gap between the predicted judgment and the actual one, and only data closes that gap. Start with low-stakes, high-frequency acts: ask one question in a meeting, send one message first, make one phone call per week instead of an email. Log what actually happened. The log is the intervention.

N5 Impulsiveness — 20 / 32 (63%) · printed label: very high

Measures: the tendency to act on urges and cravings without resisting them, particularly for food, spending, substances, sex, information and anything that relieves tension quickly.

His pattern: 20 is a genuine vulnerability, and it is the facet most likely to surprise people who know him, because it contradicts the image of the careful, quiet, self-contained man. His impulsivity is not visible; it happens in private, and it is triggered by depletion rather than by opportunity.

In daily behaviour: Late-night purchases. Sudden resignations, sudden applications, sudden messages to people he has not spoken to in years. Deciding to move, to travel, to quit a course, in an hour, after months of not deciding. Eating badly exactly on the days he is most tired. Doom-scrolling as a nervous-system sedative. A short fuse with himself when a plan requires patience.

Interactions: N5 plus C5 at 11 is the arithmetic of procrastination-then-crash: no self-discipline to start early, then a burst of impulsive all-night effort at the deadline. N5 plus C6 at 19 (mid deliberation) explains the two-mode pattern: extended analysis on decisions that feel important, immediate action on decisions that feel like relief. N5 plus N1 means the urge arrives precisely when the anxiety peaks, which is precisely when judgment is worst.

Risk: Irreversible decisions taken in a bad hour — the resignation, the large purchase, the angry message, the flight booked to escape a feeling rather than to arrive somewhere.

Practice: A single mechanical rule with no willpower in it: nothing irreversible within 72 hours of a strong feeling. Write the decision down with a date; act on the date. The rule works because it does not require him to feel differently, only to sequence differently. Second rule: remove the frictionless path — cards saved in browsers, apps one tap away, late-night access to anything he regrets.

N6 Vulnerability — 20 / 32 (63%) · printed label: very high

Measures: the capacity to cope with stress, pressure and ambiguity; the sense of being overwhelmed when demands stack. High scorers feel unable to manage, panic under load, and depend on predictability.

His pattern: At 20, his stress tolerance is thin and, crucially, it is thin in a way that shows up late. He does not panic at the start of a crisis; he copes competently, sometimes impressively, and then collapses after it. The overload is felt in the recovery, not in the event.

In daily behaviour: Two simultaneous demands feel like six. A change in plan at short notice produces disproportionate irritation. Unstructured time is not restful but vaguely threatening. He under-reports stress while it is happening and over-reports it afterwards, which means his environment constantly miscalibrates what he can take.

Interactions: N6 plus C3 at 29 is the dangerous pair: duty prevents him from saying no, and vulnerability means the accumulated yeses eventually break him. The pattern is a long period of quiet over-functioning followed by a collapse that looks sudden to everyone and was obvious to him for months. N6 plus N1 means the anticipation of overload is itself an overload.

Risk: Burnout, somatic collapse, a stress-related withdrawal (a long unexplained absence, a sudden resignation, a period of illness) that damages his reputation more than any single failure would have.

Practice: A visible capacity budget: a written number of active commitments he will hold (three, four — his number, set when calm), and a rule that a new commitment requires removing one. Plus recovery scheduled before the demanding event, not after. He will resist both, because both feel like a failure of duty, which is exactly why they need to be rules rather than choices.

5.2 Extraversion facets

E1 Warmth — 12 / 32 (38%) · printed label: very low

Measures: friendliness, affectionate behaviour, the ease of forming attachments and the tendency to show liking openly. Note that this is about expression and readiness, not about capacity for attachment.

His pattern: 12 is low but not at the floor, and the difference matters: he is not cold, he is undemonstrative. His affection exists but is delivered in a register most people do not recognise — remembering what you said six months ago, reading the thing you recommended, showing up when it matters, fixing your problem without being asked.

In daily behaviour: Neutral resting face, which strangers read as unfriendly. No small talk at the start of interactions. Discomfort with hugs, greetings rituals and verbal endearments. Rare but specific compliments, delivered flatly and therefore doubly credible. Long silences in conversation that he finds comfortable and the other person finds tense. He answers a personal question factually rather than warmly.

Interactions: E1 plus A2 at 27 is the "harsh but honest" combination: he will criticise readily and praise rarely, which exaggerates the perceived severity of his judgment. E1 plus A5 at 23 means he does not compensate with charm or self-deprecation. E1 plus O3 at 23 is the interesting part: his internal emotional life is rich. The bottleneck is entirely at expression. People who judge him by the first hour will be wrong about him; people who know him for years know a warm man behind a closed face.

Risk: Being chronically misread; a partner who feels unloved because the evidence of love is not in the expected currency; professional relationships that never become alliances.

Practice: Translate rather than transform. He does not need to become effusive; he needs to add explicit markers roughly once a week: one sentence of appreciation, one question about the other person's day, one greeting that is not purely functional. Small, mechanical, repeatable. For a man with O5 at 28 and A2 at 27, words are a reliable tool once he accepts that saying the warm thing is not dishonest.

E2 Gregariousness — 4 / 32 (13%) · printed label: very low

Measures: preference for the company of others, the number of people one wants around, and the enjoyment of crowds, parties and groups.

His pattern: This is as low as a facet gets. Four out of thirty-two means he endorsed almost no item about enjoying groups. He is not choosing solitude out of fear in this facet; he simply does not get pleasure from company in quantity.

In daily behaviour: Leaves events early or does not attend. Prefers one-to-one to any group. Finds background conversation physically tiring. Has probably developed a repertoire of polite exits. Enjoys the idea of a gathering more than the gathering. Experiences relief, not sadness, when plans are cancelled. Two hours of socialising requires an evening of recovery, and this is not negotiable by willpower.

Interactions: E2 plus N4 at 23 means group situations carry a double cost: no reward and some threat. E2 plus O5 at 28 means he prefers the company of books, and this is an honest preference, not a consolation prize. E2 plus C3 at 29 means he will still attend the obligatory events — and pay for them later, which is exactly the pattern that produces burnout in a sociable workplace.

Risk: Social atrophy — a shrinking circle that becomes a closed loop, then a single point, then none. This is the most likely route from his profile to genuine isolation, and it happens silently over years rather than in any single decision.

Practice: Choose a fixed small set rather than trying to be sociable. Three or four people, one recurring contact each per month, in formats he actually tolerates (a walk, a film, a shared task, a meal at home). The goal is not more socialising; it is the maintenance of a minimum viable social field, which is what protects N3 from becoming entrenched.

E3 Assertiveness — 4 / 32 (13%) · printed label: very low

Measures: dominance, social confidence, the tendency to take charge, speak up, lead and influence. Not aggression; the willingness to occupy space.

His pattern: Also 4. This is the single most consequential score in the profile for his life outcomes, because it governs whether his considerable ability ever becomes visible.

In daily behaviour: Does not speak in meetings unless asked directly, and then answers briefly and stops. Does not negotiate. Does not ask for a raise, a deadline extension or a better assignment. Does not correct a factual error made by a senior person in the room. Does not introduce himself. Waits to be invited into conversations. Physically takes the smallest available space — the edge of the table, the corner seat, the back row.

Interactions: E3 plus C1 at 25 is the profile's central injustice: he knows he is competent, and this knowledge never becomes claim. E3 plus N4 means the reason is not indifference but anticipated judgment. E3 plus A4 at 14 (low compliance) creates a strange hybrid: he will not lead, but he will not follow either — he simply does his own thing at his own pace, which managers read as passive resistance. E3 plus O5 at 28 means he usually has the better idea and usually does not say it, and then watches a worse idea get adopted, and then feels contempt for the process.

Risk: Systematic under-remuneration and under-promotion; being assigned the work and not the credit; a career that reflects his visibility rather than his ability; in relationships, a chronic pattern of deferring decisions that then get made for him.

Practice: Scripts, not courage. Prepare three standard sentences ("I'd like to add something", "I think the risk here is X", "I'd like to be considered for that") and use them verbatim until they stop feeling like a costume. Target: one contribution in the first ten minutes of every meeting, because the first contribution is the expensive one and later ones are cheap. Track it weekly; this is a behavioural metric, not a feeling.

E4 Activity — 7 / 32 (22%) · printed label: very low

Measures: tempo, energy, the pace of living, the preference for keeping busy, and physical briskness.

His pattern: 7 is very low. He is a slow-moving person in a fast-moving species. His natural speed is deliberate: he walks slowly, talks slowly, eats slowly, decides slowly. He does not experience this as lethargy; he experiences other people as unnecessarily hurried.

In daily behaviour: Long stretches of stillness, usually reading or thinking. Low interest in exercise for its own sake. Preference for one task at a time. Dislike of being rushed, of itineraries, of days with six appointments. Under-stimulation feels pleasant; over-stimulation feels like an assault. He can be extremely productive in a narrow band of activity and completely inert outside it.

Interactions: E4 plus O5 at 28 explains his best hours: absorbed, still, silent reading and thinking. E4 plus C5 at 11 means the physical momentum that gets most people started on tasks is missing, so tasks start late. E4 plus N3 means low mood is expressed as stillness, which makes it hard for others to distinguish his baseline from his depression — including for him.

Risk: Sedentariness with its long-term health consequences; low mood and low activity reinforcing each other into a stable low-energy equilibrium.

Practice: Do not aim at fitness culture; aim at movement that does not require motivation — walking as transport, stairs, a fixed 20 minutes at a fixed time. The evidence favours the fixed-time anchor over the ambitious plan, and a man with C5 at 11 needs the anchor, not the plan.

E5 Excitement seeking — 13 / 32 (41%) · printed label: very low

Measures: appetite for stimulation — thrills, noise, risk, bright lights, fast things, crowds, novelty of sensation.

His pattern: 13 is low. There is no thrill-seeking channel in him at all: no gambling, no dangerous driving, no extreme sports, no desire for the front row. This is protective and it is also narrowing.

In daily behaviour: Prefers quiet restaurants, quiet streets, quiet music at low volume. Does not enjoy loud gatherings, concerts, or events with crowds. Finds "fun" as commonly packaged to be actively unpleasant. His stimulation comes from information, not sensation.

Interactions: E5 plus O4 at 9 (low openness to actions) means almost no novelty entered through the body or the calendar — no new places, no new physical experiences. E5 plus O5 at 28 means the entire novelty budget is spent on ideas, which is sustainable but narrow, and narrows further with age.

Risk: A life that becomes a loop — same rooms, same routes, same inputs — which is comfortable now and depressive in ten years.

Practice: Deliberate, small, scheduled novelty that does not require thrill: one new place per month, chosen by someone else, lasting under two hours. This uses the external chooser to bypass both low E5 and low O4.

E6 Positive emotions — 15 / 32 (47%) · printed label: low

Measures: the frequency and intensity of joy, enthusiasm, excitement, pride and love. It is the facet that most directly indexes day-to-day happiness.

His pattern: 15 is just under the midpoint, and it is the highest of his Extraversion facets. That is the good news in this domain: he is not anhedonic. He feels pleasure; it is simply low-amplitude and mostly solitary, and it rarely reaches the surface where others can see it.

In daily behaviour: Quiet satisfaction rather than visible delight. A slight change in tone rather than laughter. Enthusiasm expressed as sustained attention — he will talk about a book for forty minutes without ever saying he enjoyed it. Rare, sudden, disproportionate joy in something small and well-made.

Interactions: E6 plus N3 is the main tension: positive affect is available but easily displaced by low mood. E6 plus A5 at 23 means he does not share good news, which denies him the amplification that sharing normally provides. E6 plus O2 at 26 means aesthetic experience is his most reliable route to pleasure — music, prose, design, landscape.

Risk: A life with fewer peak experiences than it could have, and a partner who never learns what makes him happy because he never says.

5.3 Openness facets

O1 Fantasy / imagination — 19 / 32 (59%) · printed label: high; sheet prints «تحمل»

Measures: the habit of daydreaming, imagining, inventing inner scenarios, and living partly in a constructed world. High scorers have a vivid interior cinema; low scorers are present- and fact-oriented.

His pattern: 19 is mid-range. He has an imagination, but it is not an escape hatch — it is a tool. He imagines arguments, structures, counterfactuals, how a system would fail, how a paragraph should be rewritten. He is less likely to invent fictional worlds for their own sake.

In daily behaviour: Long internal monologues. Preparing conversations in advance, including the other person's lines. Mental rehearsal of scenarios, usually negative ones. Reading fiction but preferring fiction with ideas in it. A tendency to drift during boring meetings into a parallel and more interesting train of thought.

Interactions: O1 plus N1 means the imagination is recruited by anxiety and runs disaster simulations rather than pleasant ones. O1 plus O5 means the simulations are well-informed and hard to dismiss. O1 plus low O4 means imagined scenarios substitute for tried ones — he has been to many places in his head and few in the world.

Risk: Substitution — the satisfaction of imagining a project replacing the work of doing it, which is the mechanism behind his unfinished-projects pattern.

Practice: Convert one imagined scenario per month into one concrete, small, timed experiment. The point is to keep imagination as a sketchpad rather than a residence.

O2 Aesthetics — 26 / 32 (81%) · printed label: very high

Measures: sensitivity to and love of beauty in art, music, poetry, nature and design; the tendency to be moved by form as well as content.

His pattern: 81% — this is one of the highest scores in the profile and probably the most reliable source of pleasure in his life. It is not a hobby; it is a perceptual mode that is always on.

In daily behaviour: Notices typography, kerning, the way a room is lit, the quality of a translation, the phrasing of a sentence. Has strong and specific taste and is quietly contemptuous of people who do not. Owns few things but cares about them. Can be stopped mid-sentence by a piece of music. Prefers one good object to five adequate ones. Is physically uncomfortable in ugly environments — fluorescent light, clutter, bad design — in a way that others find exaggerated.

Interactions: O2 plus A2 at 27 makes him an aesthetic judge who says the verdict out loud, which is socially costly and usually correct. O2 plus E1 at 12 means he does not share his enthusiasms, so this whole rich channel runs privately. O2 plus O5 means he appreciates structure as beauty — a well-made argument feels to him the way a well-made chair feels to others. O2 plus E6 at 15 is important: aesthetics is his most reliable access to positive emotion, and therefore a genuine mental-health asset that should be protected deliberately.

Risk: Contempt — an aesthetic person with low warmth and low compliance can drift into a superiority that distances him from ordinary people and ordinary pleasures.

Practice: Use it as medicine, not just as taste: a scheduled weekly dose of high-quality input (music, film, prose, a museum, a good walk in a good place), treated as non-optional as sleep. For this profile, beauty is closer to a nutrient than a luxury.

O3 Feelings — 23 / 32 (72%) · printed label: very high

Measures: openness to one's own emotional states — the capacity to feel deeply, to differentiate emotions, and to value emotional experience as such.

His pattern: 72%, and this facet resolves what would otherwise be a contradiction in his profile. He looks emotionally flat (E1 = 12, E6 = 15) but is not emotionally shallow. The interior is rich and differentiated; the exterior is closed. This is the difference between having feelings and displaying them.

In daily behaviour: Can be moved to silence by a piece of music or a paragraph. Feels things at length and in detail, and can describe them precisely if asked — which he rarely is. Cries at very specific things (usually not the things others cry at) and not at all at the things others find sad. Experiences aesthetic and moral emotions with unusual intensity.

Interactions: O3 plus A6 at 18 (low tender-mindedness) is the key tension: he feels deeply about his own experience and about form and principle, but is not moved by others' distress in the same way. That is not hypocrisy; it is a specific emotional architecture. O3 plus N3 means sadness is available in high resolution. O3 plus C3 at 29 means his emotional life is often organised around obligation rather than desire.

Risk: Emotional isolation — a rich inner life that nobody is admitted to, which over years becomes loneliness with a well-furnished interior.

Practice: Give one person access. One, not many. A weekly exchange where he says one true thing about how the week felt. For a man with A2 at 27, honesty is easy; what is hard is honesty about himself to someone who can respond.

O4 Actions — 9 / 32 (28%) · printed label: very low

Measures: willingness to try new activities, go new places, change routines, and learn by doing. It is openness expressed through behaviour rather than thought.

His pattern: 9 is low, and it is the facet that most sharply contradicts his overall Openness score. He is a very open-minded man with a very closed calendar.

In daily behaviour: Same restaurant, same order. Same route. Same holiday destination, or no holiday. Resistance to being moved, rerouted or rescheduled. New activities proposed by others are met with a long list of reasons why not. Buys the same model of everything. Would rather re-read a good book than start an unknown one.

Interactions: O4 plus O5 at 28 is the armchair-radical pattern described earlier: radical in theory, conservative in practice. O4 plus E5 at 13 means his life has very little sensory novelty in it. O4 plus C2 at 9 is a subtle point — his lack of order is not the same as love of novelty; he does not have systems, but he also does not seek new experiences. The result is not adventurous chaos; it is comfortable drift.

Risk: A life that narrows without anyone deciding to narrow it; the slow conversion of a curious mind into a fixed routine.

O5 Ideas — 28 / 32 (88%) · printed label: very high

Measures: intellectual curiosity, love of concepts, willingness to engage with abstract or theoretical material, appetite for learning for its own sake.

His pattern: 88% — his second-highest score. This is the engine of his mind and the best explanation of what he does with his evenings. Ideas are not work for him; they are the main form of pleasure.

In daily behaviour: Reads widely and constantly, across disciplines, without a syllabus. Collects explanations. Enjoys arguments about first principles. Becomes visibly animated — the only reliable animation in the profile — when a conversation turns conceptual. Interactions: O5 plus O6 at 28 makes him both curious and unorthodox, a combination that produces genuinely independent thinking and a certain amount of friction with institutions. O5 plus A2 at 27 means he corrects errors, including senior people's, which is a career hazard. O5 plus E3 at 4 means the ideas stay in his head. Practice: Attach one output to the input. For every three books, one page of written synthesis; for every idea, one note that could be shown to a stranger. The point is not productivity theatre; it is that his ideas currently have no route to the world, and one route, however small, changes what the whole trait is worth.

O6 Values — 28 / 32 (88%) · printed label: very high

Measures: readiness to re-examine social, political, religious and moral values; willingness to accept that authority and tradition may be wrong.

His pattern: 88%, tied with O5 as his highest Openness facet and one of the three highest scores in the whole protocol. He is not merely open to new ideas; he is constitutionally unable to treat a received rule as self-justifying.

In daily behaviour: Asks "why is it done this way?" about things nobody asks about. Is unmoved by "that's how we've always done it". Holds positions that are his own, sometimes uncomfortably so. Does not join groups, parties, or causes, because joining requires accepting a package. Is respectful of individuals and sceptical of institutions. May retain a private spirituality while rejecting its institutional forms, or reject both.

Interactions: O6 plus A1 at 17 (low trust) is the combination that makes him a genuine sceptic rather than a mere liberal: he distrusts both tradition and the people who claim to replace it. O6 plus C3 at 29 is the most interesting tension in the profile: he is dutiful toward obligations and unconvinced by conventions. He will fulfil the requirement and refuse the belief behind it. That is a stable and honourable position, and it is exhausting to live in an institution that wants both.

Risk: Chronic friction in hierarchical settings; being labelled difficult or ideological when he is merely unconvinced; isolation from communities that would otherwise support him.

Practice: Distinguish, explicitly, between obligations he keeps and beliefs he withholds. Written down, this distinction protects him: it lets him comply where compliance is cheap, and reserve resistance for where it matters, instead of leaking resistance into everything.

5.4 Agreeableness facets

A1 Trust — 17 / 32 (53%) · printed label: low

Measures: the default assumption about other people's intentions — benevolent or self-interested, safe or risky.

His pattern: 17 is just over the midpoint arithmetically, but the printed key calls it low, and behaviourally the low reading fits better: his default is suspended judgment rather than open welcome. He does not assume bad intent; he declines to assume good intent.

In daily behaviour: Reads the fine print. Asks what the catch is. Notices when a compliment is doing work. Is slow to believe an institution's promises and vindicated often enough to keep doing it. Does not lend money casually, does not co-sign, does not vouch for people he has not tested. Is loyal to individuals who have earned it, and almost inert toward those who have not.

Interactions: A1 plus O6 at 28 makes him sceptical of both received authority and fashionable alternatives, which is a rare and defensible intellectual position. A1 plus N1 means distrust is emotionally expensive — he does not enjoy suspicion, he simply cannot switch it off. A1 plus A2 at 27 means he says what he thinks of people's motives, which is how a cautious man acquires a reputation for being harsh.

Risk: Slow relationship formation; missed opportunities that required a small leap of faith; a self-confirming loop in which low trust produces guarded behaviour that produces guarded responses.

Practice: Disclose a small vulnerability early and observe. Trust is built by testable increments, not by deciding to be trusting. One low-stakes test per new relationship, with the result recorded, turns a global attitude into an evidence-based practice.

A2 Straightforwardness — 27 / 32 (84%) · printed label: very high

Measures: candour, frankness, sincerity, and the avoidance of manipulation, flattery and indirection.

His pattern: 84% — his fourth-highest score, and the one that shapes his social reputation more than any other. He does not have a diplomatic register. It is not that he chooses truth over tact; it is that tact does not occur to him as an option until after the sentence has landed.

In daily behaviour: Delivers evaluations without preamble. Answers "how are you?" accurately. Says "I don't know" instead of improvising. Says "that's wrong" instead of "there may be an issue with". Does not flatter, does not soften, does not use the passive voice to hide the agent. Is also, importantly, straightforward about his own difficulties — he will admit ignorance, error and confusion faster than most people, which makes him unusually trustworthy to those who know him.

Interactions: A2 plus E1 at 12 means there is no warm packaging around the truth. A2 plus A5 at 23 means the truth is never self-serving, which is the only reason it is tolerated. A2 plus O5 at 28 means the truths are informed. A2 plus N2 at 16 means occasional irritation leaks into the delivery. A2 plus A3 at 21 (modest altruism) means he will give you an accurate assessment without necessarily offering to fix the problem, which reads as cold even when he would fix it if asked.

Risk: Being right and disliked; damaging relationships with people who needed support rather than accuracy; in a hierarchical workplace, being classified as a problem.

Practice: The one-sentence addition. Not softening the content, but adding a sentence that locates him on the other person's side ("I'd rather you hear this from me"). This costs nothing in honesty and buys most of the goodwill he currently forgoes. Second practice: ask permission before evaluating ("do you want my honest read, or do you want sympathy?"). It converts an assault into a service.

A3 Altruism — 21 / 32 (66%) · printed label: low

Measures: active concern for others' welfare, generosity of time and effort, willingness to help without reward.

His pattern: 21 is moderate — the printed "low" label overstates it. The right reading is conditional helpfulness: he helps when asked, when it is his duty, or when the person has earned it. He does not scan for people to rescue.

In daily behaviour: Will spend three hours solving a colleague's technical problem if asked directly. Will not notice that the colleague needed help. Will give money to a specific person in a specific crisis and not to a general appeal. Prefers to help in ways that involve competence rather than presence: fixing, explaining, drafting, reviewing. Dislikes emotional labour and performs it awkwardly.

Interactions: A3 plus A6 at 18 means his help is practical rather than comforting. A3 plus A2 at 27 means he will tell you what you need to hear rather than what supports you. A3 plus C3 at 29 means duty-based help is unlimited while discretionary help is scarce — a strange but consistent pattern, and one that makes him reliable in roles with defined obligations and unavailable in roles that require spontaneous generosity.

Risk: Being perceived as self-absorbed; a partner who carries the emotional and domestic load because he does not volunteer.

Practice: One proactive act of help per week, offered before it is requested and small enough not to be resented. For this profile, the obstacle is not willingness but noticing, so the practice should be a scheduled question to one specific person: "what do you need this week?"

A4 Compliance — 14 / 32 (44%) · printed label: low

Measures: the tendency to defer, yield, smooth over conflict, and accommodate others' wishes; the inverse face of aggression and stubbornness.

His pattern: 14 is low, and this is one of the most consequential facets for how he is experienced at work and in family life. He does not yield to pressure. He is not aggressive about it; he simply does not move.

In daily behaviour: Does not adopt other people's framing under social pressure. Answers "we'll see" to requests he intends to decline. Does not apologise to end a conversation. Does not change a technical judgment because a senior person disagrees. Does exactly what was agreed and no more, which is a form of resistance with plausible deniability. In family conflicts, becomes silent rather than compliant — and then does what he had decided to do all along.

Interactions: A4 plus E3 at 4 produces the passive-resistance pattern in its purest form: no confrontation, no concession. A4 plus A2 at 27 means the disagreement is usually stated plainly at least once. A4 plus C3 at 29 means he is compliant with obligations and non-compliant with preferences — he will do everything the job requires and nothing the boss merely wants. A4 plus O6 at 28 means the non-compliance has an intellectual basis, not merely a temperamental one.

Risk: Being managed out of roles by people who read non-compliance as disloyalty; in relationships, an invisible arms race in which he resists silently and the partner escalates.

Practice: Make disagreement explicit and early, in one sentence, instead of letting it become silent non-cooperation. "I don't think that's the right approach, and here's why — but I'll do it your way if you decide." This preserves his autonomy and removes the appearance of sabotage. It is also more honest, which he values.

A5 Modesty — 23 / 32 (72%) · printed label: very high

Measures: humility, dislike of self-promotion, unwillingness to claim superiority or attention.

His pattern: 72% — a genuine strength and a genuine liability, depending on what the situation requires. He does not boast, does not name-drop, does not angle for credit, and is visibly uncomfortable when praised in public.

In daily behaviour: Downplays his knowledge ("I read something about that once"), even when he is the most informed person in the room. Deflects compliments. Lets others take credit without correction. Prefers roles without titles and without spotlights. Would rather be underestimated than admired — partly humility, partly N4 avoidance of judgment.

Interactions: A5 plus E3 at 4 is the compounding pair: no assertiveness and no self-promotion means his competence is structurally invisible. A5 plus A2 at 27 is what makes his bluntness forgivable, because it is obviously not self-serving. A5 plus C1 at 25 is the odd combination of knowing he is capable and refusing to say so — which produces a private, corrosive gap between self-estimate and public record.

Risk: A working life in which less capable people are promoted past him and he says nothing; a slow accumulation of contempt for a system that rewards display.

Practice: Bounded self-advocacy: keep a written record of what he actually did (not for boasting, for evidence), and share it only where the format requires it — annual reviews, applications, introductions. Modesty can coexist with accurate reporting; the trick is to treat the report as data, not as a claim of superiority.

A6 Tender-mindedness — 18 / 32 (56%) · printed label: very low

Measures: sympathy, sentimentality, being moved by others' distress, and the tendency to let feelings about people shape judgments.

His pattern: 18 sits just above the midpoint, though the sheet labels it "very low". Behaviourally the low reading is more informative: his sympathy is deliberative, not reflexive. He is not moved automatically; he decides whether a case deserves sympathy, and sometimes decides it does not.

In daily behaviour: Responds to a friend's crisis with a plan, not with tears. Is unmoved by sentimentality in advertising, politics and family ritual. Argues that hard decisions should be made on the merits even when someone is hurt. Can be surprising, in a good way, when the case does deserve sympathy — he will then act decisively and generously, having concluded rather than felt his way there.

Interactions: A6 plus O3 at 23 is the apparent contradiction: a man with a rich emotional life who is not softened by others' emotions. Both are true and they are separable constructs. A6 plus A1 at 17 means the low sympathy blends into general scepticism about claims of victimhood, which can read as harshness in a workplace or family that runs on appeals to feeling. A6 plus C3 at 29 means that when principle and sympathy conflict, principle wins — consistently and predictably.

Risk: The reputation of a cold man; a partner or child who needs comfort and receives analysis; moral rigidity in situations where flexibility would be kinder.

Practice: Separate the two responses explicitly, in words: "here's what I think you should do — and here's me being sorry this happened." The second sentence costs nothing and supplies what A6 does not do automatically. For someone with A2 at 27, saying it plainly is easier than performing it.

5.5 Conscientiousness facets

C1 Competence — 25 / 32 (78%) · printed label: very high

Measures: the sense of being capable, effective and prepared; belief in one's own ability to handle tasks well.

His pattern: 78% — high, and it is the only facet in the profile that contradicts the general self-deprecating tone of his Agreeableness and Neuroticism. He knows he can do the work. The self-doubt is social and evaluative, not technical.

In daily behaviour: Quiet confidence in his own domain. Volunteers for difficult technical problems in private, avoids presenting them in public. Judges others' work by a standard he applies to himself. Is impatient with incompetence, and more impatient with confident incompetence. Dislikes being managed on method by people who understand the work less well — which is a large share of the grid, and a reliable source of N2 irritation.

Interactions: C1 plus E3 at 4 is the profile's central waste: capability that never becomes claim. C1 plus A5 at 23 means he will not state the gap, and will not correct other people's underestimation of him. C1 plus O5 at 28 means the competence is broad, not just procedural. C1 plus N4 means the confidence evaporates specifically in front of an audience — he is competent in the room alone and awkward in the room with people.

Risk: Contempt for colleagues and superiors accumulating quietly; refusal to accept direction from anyone he judges less competent, expressed as passive resistance.

Practice: Translate competence into artefacts — documentation, a short write-up, a system that survives him. Competence that only exists in his own head cannot be paid for, promoted, or protected.

C2 Order — 9 / 32 (28%) · printed label: very low

Measures: organisation, tidiness, method, planning, and the preference for systems that keep things in their place.

His pattern: 28% — very low, and it is the facet that most contradicts his dutifulness. The sheet's own arithmetic shows the split: C3 at 91% and C2 at 28% in the same person.

In daily behaviour: Desk covered in layers of paper. Files named "final2". Bills paid late but paid. Library books returned after the due date, with the fine paid without complaint. Plans made in his head and not written down, so half are forgotten. Arrives on time for things that matter and forgets things that do not. Loses objects constantly and finds them in the last place he looked, three weeks later.

Interactions: C2 plus C3 is the defining tension: he will not let you down and he will not be organised about it. C2 plus C5 at 11 means there is no discipline to substitute for system. C2 plus O5 at 28 means his mental filing is excellent and his physical filing is nonexistent — he can retrieve a reference he read in 2015 but not the form he signed last week. C2 plus N1 means the disorder is a persistent low-grade guilt.

Risk: Administrative failures that damage him disproportionately in bureaucratic environments; missed opportunities that expired while unfiled; domestic conflict over mess.

Practice: Do not attempt to become orderly; outsource the order. Two mechanisms only: a single capture point (one notebook or one app where every commitment lands immediately) and an external calendar with alarms for anything with a date. This works because it does not require C2 to rise; it routes around it.

C3 Dutifulness — 29 / 32 (91%) · printed label: very high

Measures: the strength of felt obligation — keeping promises, honouring commitments, fulfilling the requirements of one's role even when inconvenient or unobserved.

His pattern: 91%, his highest score. This is the moral spine of the profile and the trait he would probably name if asked what he is.

In daily behaviour: Does what he said he would. Returns things borrowed. Completes work nobody will check. Stays in obligations long past the point where leaving would be reasonable. Cannot bring himself to half-do a task. Feels guilt with disproportionate intensity over small failures of reliability, and comparatively little over failures of warmth — a revealing asymmetry.

Interactions: C3 plus high N is the burnout mechanism: duty says yes, vulnerability pays for it later, and no one is told. C3 plus A4 at 14 is a strange and important pair: he is not compliant but he is dutiful; he will not obey you, but he will not abandon the obligation either. C3 plus O6 at 28 means the duty is to the substance of the commitment, not to the authority behind it. C3 plus C4 at 10 means the duty is not in service of ambition — there is no career climb justifying it, only the commitment itself.

Risk: Exploitation by people who notice that he never refuses; staying years too long in bad situations; moral burnout, in which duty is finally experienced as a cage and the reaction is disproportionate (sudden resignation, abrupt withdrawal).

Practice: Make duty finite and defined. A commitment should have a scope and an end date; open-ended obligations are what break this profile. And add one duty to himself, written down with the same seriousness as the others — this is the only mechanism that has a chance against a 91% score.

C4 Achievement striving — 10 / 32 (31%) · printed label: very low

Measures: ambition, the drive toward high standards, advancement, excellence as a goal, and the effort spent pursuing more.

His pattern: 31%. He has essentially no career engine. This is not laziness and not depression; it is an absence of appetite for the rewards that drive most achievement behaviour — status, money, recognition, comparison.

In daily behaviour: Does not seek promotion. Does not benchmark himself against peers, and is genuinely unimpressed by other people's titles. Does excellent work when the task requires excellence and does not require excellence of himself in general. Declines opportunities that would mean more responsibility, and is puzzled when others find this strange. Would rather have free time than a larger salary.

Interactions: C4 plus E3 at 4 means no push from inside and no push from outside. C4 plus C1 at 25 is the profile's most frustrating combination: the ability to be excellent combined with the absence of any desire to be seen as excellent. C4 plus O5 at 28 means the effort goes into learning rather than into advancement, which is why his knowledge is deep and his CV thin. C4 plus A5 at 23 means he genuinely does not want the spotlight, so this is not self-sabotage — it is a values position.

Risk: Underemployment relative to ability for a whole career; financial precarity in later life; regret arriving at fifty-five rather than at forty.

Practice: Reframe from ambition to stewardship: he will not pursue status, but he may accept responsibility for a body of work. One deliberately chosen "thing I am the custodian of" — a project, a function, a publication — is achievable for someone who will not chase a title. This detour around C4 works far better than trying to install ambition.

C5 Self-discipline — 11 / 32 (34%) · printed label: very low

Measures: the capacity to begin tasks, persist through boredom and complete work without external pressure; the ability to make oneself do what one has decided.

His pattern: 34%. He cannot self-start on anything uninteresting, and he knows it. The gap between intention and action is wide and predictable.

In daily behaviour: Deadline-driven work: long fallow periods and then intense, competent, last-minute output. Abandoned courses, unread books bought with enthusiasm, subscriptions cancelled after two uses. Good intentions that survive until the first obstacle. A private self-image of being someone who "works better under pressure", which is true and also a description of a constraint rather than a strategy.

Interactions: C5 plus C3 at 29 explains why the pattern is not catastrophic: when the deadline is a commitment to another person, duty takes over and the work gets done. When it is a promise to himself, nothing happens. C5 plus N5 at 20 means distraction wins in the moment of temptation. C5 plus E4 at 7 means there is no surplus physical energy to force the issue. C5 plus O5 at 28 means he will happily read for six hours and cannot make himself write for twenty minutes.

Risk: Projects that never ship; health behaviours that never start; the slow accumulation of a life of potential.

Practice: Use duty as the engine, since it is the only working one: make every important personal goal a commitment to a person with a date (a weekly check-in, a co-author, a class with a teacher). Add a 15-minute daily slot at a fixed time for the one thing he keeps not doing. For this profile, external scaffolding is not a crutch; it is the only mechanism that reliably works.

C6 Deliberation — 19 / 32 (59%) · printed label: average

Measures: care and caution in decision-making — thinking before acting, considering consequences, avoiding haste.

His pattern: 59%, mid-range, and this is the facet that produces his most confusing behaviour, because it coexists with N5 impulsiveness at 63%.

In daily behaviour: Long, careful, sometimes excessive consideration of anything that feels consequential — a purchase, a job, a message, a plan — with research, comparison and delay. Then, on something that matters less or feels like relief, an immediate and unconsidered act. He is simultaneously the man who took three months to choose a phone and the man who booked a flight at midnight.

Interactions: C6 plus N5 is the two-mode pattern; the trigger for switching from deliberation to impulse is emotional depletion, not decision importance. C6 plus N1 means deliberation is extended by worry rather than by thoroughness, so decisions are late for reasons that have nothing to do with their difficulty. C6 plus C5 at 11 means the deliberation does not convert into planning, only into postponement.

Risk: Decisions made in the wrong mode — over-deliberated on small things, under-deliberated on large ones.

Practice: Set the mode in advance by size, not by feeling: decisions under a stated threshold get a ten-minute limit; decisions above it get a forty-eight-hour waiting period and one written page of pros and cons. This replaces a fluctuating internal state with a fixed rule.

6. Twelve mechanisms: how the facets combine into a person

Facet dossiers describe parts. Personality is in the joints. What follows are twelve named mechanisms, each built from a specific combination of scores, each with a behavioural signature and a consequence. These are the load-bearing explanations for everything in the sections on behaviour, career and relationships.

Mechanism 1 — The invisible competence loop (C1 25 + E3 4 + A5 23 + N4 23)

He is capable and knows it, and he will not say so, and he fears being judged, and he does not claim space. Trace the loop: a task arrives, he does it well, nobody sees the quality because he delivered it quietly and did not present it, the invisibility confirms his belief that presentation is for other people, and the next task arrives with no added recognition. Over years the loop produces a man whose actual ability and whose recorded ability have drifted apart.

The loop has a second arm that is worse. Because his competence is invisible, he is given work below his level, which he does well and finds boring, which reduces his engagement, which reduces his visible output, which confirms the low expectations. Under-utilisation is the most likely long-run outcome of this profile, and it is not caused by any single score. It is caused by the interaction.

Break point: any external structure that makes quality visible without requiring him to boast — a written record, a reviewer who names him, a format where the work speaks (a document rather than a meeting).

Mechanism 2 — The honesty tax (A2 27 + E1 12 + A6 18 + N2 16)

Truth delivered without warmth, without softness and with occasional irritation is taxed at a rate that is invisible to the person paying it. He says a correct thing; the room registers a cold thing; he is treated slightly worse; he cannot see the connection because the content of what he said was right.

The tax compounds in a specific way: the more accurate he is, the more he is resented, so the incentive structure rewards him for being less accurate, which he will refuse to do, which further raises the tax. This is how a principled man acquires a reputation for being difficult in an organisation that says it values candour.

Break point: one added sentence of affiliation per critical statement. It reduces the tax dramatically and costs nothing in accuracy. The obstacle is not skill but the belief that packaging is a form of dishonesty — which is worth interrogating, because he already packages in other domains (he does not tell strangers everything he thinks of them).

Mechanism 3 — Duty minus order (C3 29 + C2 9 + C5 11)

The strongest single mechanism in his working life. Duty guarantees that the important things get done; disorder and low discipline guarantee that they get done late, painfully, and with collateral administrative damage. He is the colleague who delivers a genuinely excellent report on the last possible afternoon and has not answered the three emails that would have made the report easier to receive.

The mechanism has an emotional cost that is easy to miss: because duty is high and administration is low, he lives with a permanent small deficit of unfinished obligations. There is always something overdue, and the N3/N1 pair converts "something overdue" into "I am failing".

Break point: external systems rather than internal reform. One capture point for commitments, one calendar with alarms, one weekly 20-minute admin slot — small, mechanical, non-aspirational.

Mechanism 4 — Analysis then leap (C6 19 + N5 20 + N1 21)

Two decision modes, switched by emotion rather than by stakes. In the deliberate mode he researches exhaustively, compares, postpones, and produces a decision that is late and defensible. In the impulsive mode he acts within minutes on something that promises relief, and the consequences arrive later.

The distribution of the two modes is counter-intuitive: the big, consequential, slow-burning decisions (career, marriage, emigration) are the ones he deliberates on for years, and the smaller but irreversible ones (quitting, buying, sending, booking) are the ones he takes in an hour. That is exactly backwards from the standpoint of risk, and it is predictable from the scores: deliberation is triggered by perceived importance, and impulse is triggered by depletion.

Break point: a written 72-hour rule for irreversible acts, plus a pre-commitment for the big decisions (a deadline by which he must choose, agreed with someone else).

Mechanism 5 — The low-temperature empathy (A6 18 + A3 21 + O3 23 + A1 17)

He feels his own emotions vividly (O3) and other people's weakly (A6), helps when asked rather than when moved (A3), and begins from scepticism about motives (A1). The result is a person who can be relied upon in a crisis of substance and cannot be relied upon in a crisis of feeling. Give him a broken car, a legal problem, a technical failure, a difficult decision and he is excellent: calm, practical, generous with his competence. Give him a bereavement, a rejection, a shame and he will offer a solution and then go quiet.

This asymmetry is the single most likely source of long-run damage in his intimate relationships, because intimate relationships run mainly on the second kind of crisis.

Break point: a rule of sequence — acknowledge first, solve second, always in that order. Acknowledging takes one sentence and can be prepared in advance, which suits a man who prefers prepared sentences to spontaneous warmth.

Mechanism 6 — The social battery and its debts (E2 4 + E4 7 + N6 20 + N4 23)

Small capacity, slow recharge, high cost, and a strong aversion to being observed while depleted. He attends the event, performs adequately, and then pays for it with two days of flatness that he does not attribute to the event because the causal link is invisible to him. So he does not learn to budget, and the debt accumulates.

There is a specific trap here: because N6 (vulnerability) is high, the collapse happens after the demand, so his environment concludes he copes well. And because E1 is low, his recovery looks like coldness or sulking rather than exhaustion. Both misreadings make it harder for him to negotiate the recovery time he needs.

Break point: naming the arithmetic out loud to the people involved ("I'll come for an hour and then I'll need to leave"), and scheduling recovery as an appointment rather than hoping for it.

Mechanism 7 — Unconventional values, conventional habits (O6 28 + O5 28 + O4 9 + C3 29)

He thinks like a radical and lives like a clerk. His opinions about how things should be are far outside the mainstream; his actual behaviour is more conventional than most people's, because duty and habit dominate. This is not hypocrisy; it is a division of labour between the mind and the calendar, and it is stable.

The social consequence is that people who know his opinions find him surprising, and people who know his routine find him conservative, and neither group has the full picture. The personal consequence is a quiet sense of living a life smaller than his ideas.

Break point: one deliberate annual experiment that puts a belief into action in a small way. The point is not lifestyle change; it is closing the gap between the two halves enough that the ideas do not become purely decorative.

Mechanism 8 — The duty cage (C3 29 + N6 20 + E3 4 + N3 20)

Because duty outranks wellbeing, he stays. Bad job, bad city, bad relationship, bad arrangement — he stays, because leaving would be a failure of an obligation, and obligation is the one thing he cannot negotiate. The exit, when it comes, is usually not a decision but a collapse: an abrupt resignation, a withdrawal, a period of illness, a sudden move.

This is the mechanism most worth watching. It is also the mechanism that most reliably produces regret at midlife, because the years of staying are invisible while they happen.

Break point: an explicit, written re-evaluation date for every long commitment (a year, two years), at which staying becomes an active choice rather than a default. Duty to an obligation that has changed is not the same as duty to the obligation he originally accepted, and he can be persuaded by that argument because it is logical.

Mechanism 9 — Aesthetic solitude (O2 26 + O3 23 + E2 4 + E6 15)

His most reliable joy is solitary and aesthetic: music, prose, a well-made film, a quiet room, a landscape. This is a genuinely good thing — it is a self-supplying source of meaning that does not depend on other people, money or status. It is also a trap, because it makes solitude comfortable enough that he never has to solve the loneliness problem.

Over decades, the mechanism produces a man with an extremely rich private life and an extremely thin public one; content on many evenings and unaccountably sad on certain birthdays.

Break point: share a fraction of it. One piece of music sent to one person, one film watched with someone, one book discussed. Aesthetic experience scales badly in isolation; it is designed to be transmitted.

Mechanism 10 — The half-life of ambition (C4 10 + E3 4 + A5 23 + O5 28)

He learns for the pleasure of learning and stops there. There is no engine converting knowledge into position, publication, product, or pay. The half-life of an ambition for him is short: it appears, is enjoyed, is researched, and then quietly expires without ever being announced.

The cost is not laziness but opportunity: decades of capability that never became a body of work, while less capable and more assertive people accumulated the visible record.

Break point: one output channel, chosen for pleasure rather than for advancement, with a schedule. Teaching one evening class, writing one short piece a month, maintaining one small public project. Something that turns the input into output without requiring the ambition he does not have.

Mechanism 11 — The private critic (N4 23 + N3 20 + A5 23 + C1 25)

He is his own harshest audience and his most understated advocate, simultaneously. The internal critic has high standards (C1), a full record of his failures (N3), a strong sense of what is presumptuous (A5) and a constant awareness of being seen (N4). The result is a man who can be genuinely proud of something and unable to say so, and genuinely ashamed of something trivial and unable to stop thinking about it.

This is the mechanism behind the mismatch between how he is seen (quiet, competent, slightly cold) and how he feels (inadequate, exposed, tired).

Break point: external calibration. The critic cannot be argued with internally, but it can be contradicted by data: ask one person per quarter for an honest read of his performance, and write down the answer. Unpleasant as it is, the answer is always less harsh than the internal version, and that discrepancy is the only available lever.

Mechanism 12 — The narrowing corridor (O4 9 + E5 13 + E2 4 + N3 20)

Low openness to action, low sensation seeking, low gregariousness and a proneness to low mood combine into a slow, self-reinforcing narrowing of life: same places, same people, same inputs, fewer and fewer novel stimuli, and a mood that needs novel stimuli to lift.

This is the most insidious mechanism in the profile because nothing dramatic happens. There is no crisis, no decision, no event. There is simply a life that is a little smaller each year, and a man who cannot say when it started.

Break point: scheduled, externally chosen, low-cost novelty — one thing per month, decided by someone else, under two hours long. This is the single highest-leverage preventive intervention in the entire document, precisely because it works against the grain of four facets at once.

7. Behaviour, setting by setting

This section converts the mechanisms into predictions. Each prediction is stated so that it can be checked against reality — if most of them are wrong, the reading of the protocol is wrong and should be revised. The confidence labels are my own: high where several facets agree, medium where the inference is one step long, soft where it is an extrapolation.

7.1 At work: the shape of a working day

Morning. Slow start, high competence. He arrives, does not greet the room warmly, and takes twenty to forty minutes to get into the work. His best cognitive hours are probably late morning, and he protects them poorly because he answers whatever arrives first. (High.)

The desk. Physical disorder with mental order. Layers of paper, several half-finished documents, a mug from yesterday, a notebook he cannot find. He can locate any file on the network and no form on his desk. (High: C2 9.)

Task selection. He gravitates to whatever is most interesting and postpones whatever is most administrative. If the interesting task is also the important one, he is excellent. If the important task is boring, it will be done at the deadline in one long competent burst. (High: O5 28, C5 11, C3 29.)

Meetings. Arrives prepared, says little, notices the flaw in the plan, does not raise it in the room, and afterwards is quietly angry that the flaw was adopted. If asked directly he gives a precise, complete and slightly blunt answer. He contributes most in the five minutes after the meeting, one-to-one, to whoever is willing to listen. (High: E3 4, N4 23, A2 27, C1 25.)

Email. His best medium. Short, exact, unadorned, occasionally too short to be polite. Answers the question asked and not the question implied. Will send a two-line reply where four paragraphs of reassurance were expected. Rarely uses exclamation marks. Delays replies to anything emotionally loaded by a day or two. (High.)

Phone calls. Avoids them. Lets them ring, then texts. Prepares notes for calls. Dislikes the absence of editing time, which is the real reason — a call is a conversation he cannot revise. (High: N4 + A2.)

Deadlines. Two kinds, with opposite outcomes. Deadlines owed to another person: met, sometimes at personal cost, and the work is good (C3 29). Deadlines owed to himself: missed silently and indefinitely (C5 11). (High.)

Feedback. Gives it accurately and without cushioning; receives it with disproportionate weight on the negative item. Will remember one critical sentence for years and will not mention it. (High: A2 27, N3 20.)

Authority. Loyal to the role, sceptical of the person. Will comply with the requirements of the position and will not adopt the preferences of the holder. Will not flatter, will not manoeuvre, will not attend the optional social event. Managers tend to find him either a relief (no drama, no politics, work delivered) or an irritant (does not consult, does not defer, does not visibly care). (High: A4 14, O6 28, E3 4.)

Colleagues. Respected for competence, not loved. The relationship he has with most colleagues is functional and stable; the ones he actually likes are few and are known for years. He is the person others go to with a difficult technical problem and not the person others go to with a personal one. (High.)

Under pressure. Competent in the event, depleted afterwards. During a crisis he is calm, focused and unusually good; two days later he is flat, withdrawn and inexplicably tired. His environment will read this as a mood swing. (Medium-high: N6 20.)

When bored. The most dangerous state for him, more dangerous than stress. Boredom in this profile produces drift, then private contempt, then an impulsive decision (a resignation, an application, a new project) taken at the moment of lowest judgment. (Medium-high: O5 28, C4 10, N5 20.)

7.2 Communication style

Register. Dry, precise, low-affect. Complete sentences, correct grammar, no filler. Uses technical vocabulary naturally and does not simplify unless asked. Humour is ironic, often deadpan, sometimes mistaken for seriousness. (High: O5, A2, E1 low.)

Structure. He leads with the conclusion and expects others to do the same. Long preambles irritate him. He interrupts people who are circling a point, which is read as impatience and is, in fact, impatience. (Medium-high: N2 16, A4 14.)

Silences. Comfortable. He will let a silence run past the point where others feel obliged to fill it. He is not being hostile; he is thinking, or he has nothing to add and sees no reason to perform. (High: E2 4.)

What he will not do. Small talk beyond the minimum. Compliments he does not mean. Apologies designed to end a conversation. Statements of feeling in public. Self-promotion of any kind. Asking for help in front of others. (High.)

What he does better than most. Explaining a complex thing in writing. Saying the difficult true sentence that everyone else is avoiding. Admitting error. Answering a question with "I don't know" instead of fabrication. Remembering what a person actually said. (High.)

7.3 Conflict

His conflict signature is withdrawal plus truth plus non-compliance, and it has four stages. (1) He notices the problem early and says nothing, because saying it would require an emotional register he does not have. (2) If pressed, he states the disagreement plainly, once, without softening. (3) He does not repeat it and does not escalate; he simply stops cooperating with the part he disagrees with. (4) If the pressure continues, he removes himself — from the project, the conversation, the room, eventually the job or the relationship — and the removal looks sudden to everyone else and was decided long before.

What he almost never does: shout, insult, threaten, manipulate, gossip, retaliate, or hold a grudge actively. His N2 is the lowest of his N facets; hostility is not his channel. His grudges are passive and long.

The failure mode to watch is the fourth stage. Because he announces nothing and the withdrawal is quiet, the other party is usually surprised by the end, and he is usually unable to explain the sequence, because from his side it was obvious for months. (High.)

7.4 Decision-making

Small decisions: quick, sometimes impulsive, especially when tired. (Medium: N5.)

Medium decisions: researched well past the point of usefulness. He will read nine reviews of a thing that has three relevant differences. (Medium-high: C6 19, N1 21, O5 28.)

Large decisions: postponed, sometimes for years, until external circumstances or an emotional surge forces the issue. The choice, when it comes, is often made in a single afternoon after a long period of not deciding. (Medium-high: C6 + N5 + C5.)

Criterion. He optimises for coherence and correctness, not for happiness or for other people's approval. Asked to choose between an option that is right and an option that is pleasant, he will take the right one and be quietly unhappy about it. (Medium: C3 29, O6 28, C4 10.)

Regret pattern. He regrets action rarely and inaction often. This is unusual and worth knowing: most people regret their impulsive choices more than their omissions, but with high C3 and high O5, the things he did not do — the book not written, the job not taken, the person not asked — weigh more than the things he did badly. (Soft, but consistent with the profile.)

7.5 Money

Earning. Adequate and stable rather than maximised. Low C4 means he does not chase salary; low E3 means he does not negotiate it; A5 means he does not believe he deserves more than he gets. The predictable outcome is being paid below market for years. (High.)

Spending. Modest baseline with occasional impulsive spikes. He will not buy status objects, will spend real money on a good object he will keep for a decade, and will occasionally make an unplanned purchase at midnight that he half-regrets. (Medium-high: A5 23, O2 26, N5 20.)

Administration. The weak point. Bills paid late, receipts lost, tax filed at the deadline, savings unmanaged. Not because he cannot understand finance — he can, better than most — but because the ongoing administration of money is exactly the kind of task his C2/C5 pattern cannot sustain. (High.)

Risk posture. Conservative. Low E5 (no thrill), low O4 (no novelty), high N1 (fear of loss). He will not gamble and will not invest aggressively. The real financial risk in his life is not a bad bet; it is inertia and under-earning. (High.)

7.6 Learning

He is an autodidact by temperament: wide, deep, unsystematic. He learns by reading and by arguing, not by courses. He will finish a book that interests him in two days and abandon a course he paid for in week two. He retains concepts and structures and loses administrative details. He prefers primary sources to summaries and original arguments to textbooks. He learns best with a problem to solve and worst with a syllabus to complete.

Two consequences. First, his knowledge is real and unusual: he will know things his colleagues do not, in domains nobody assigned him. Second, he has no credentials for most of it, which means it cannot be converted into position without an intermediary (a person who introduces him, a portfolio, a publication). (High: O5 28, C5 11.)

7.7 Health and the body

His health profile is the classic sedentary internaliser. Low E4 (activity 7) and low E5 (excitement seeking 13) mean very little spontaneous movement; high N1 and N6 mean a body that carries tension; high N3 means mood-related dips in self-care; low C5 means health routines start and die. The predictable set of issues, in order of likelihood: sleep-onset insomnia (worry at bedtime), chronic neck and shoulder tension, digestive complaints that no test explains, low aerobic fitness, weight gain concentrated in the abdomen after thirty-five, and dental and medical check-ups skipped because nobody chases them. (High confidence on the behavioural pattern, medium on any specific condition.)

The protective factors are real too: he does not drink heavily by temperament (though N5 plus N3 makes evening alcohol a plausible self-medication and the one substance risk worth naming), does not take physical risks, does not drive fast, and is unlikely to ignore a symptom that frightens him — high N1 produces health anxiety, which is unpleasant but does bring people to doctors.

Sleep. Probably his most reliable indicator. Late to bed, reads or scrolls, falls asleep with a mind still running, wakes at three, functions on less sleep than he needs, compensates with caffeine, repeats. Fixing sleep would improve N1, N3, N2 and C5 simultaneously, which makes it the highest-leverage single change available to him.

7.8 Appearance, home, possessions

Appearance. Unshowy and considered. Probably a small wardrobe of things he actually likes, worn until they wear out, in dark and neutral colours, with an aesthetic logic nobody else notices (O2 26) and a total absence of status display (A5 23). Could read as careless to people who equate effort with presentation.

Home. Two zones: one carefully arranged and one chaotic. The arranged zone is usually where he reads, listens or works — a good chair, good light, a shelf ordered by a logic of his own. The chaotic zone is wherever paper and objects accumulate. Decorated slowly, with objects that have meaning, and with a strong preference for quiet over display. (Medium-high: O2 + C2 9.)

Possessions. Few, chosen, kept. He would rather own one good knife than six adequate ones. Does not upgrade compulsively, does not follow gadget cycles, is suspicious of novelty in objects (O4 9) while being extremely receptive to novelty in ideas (O5 28).

7.9 Friendship

Quantity: very few. Probably two to four people he would call friends, and a wider ring of acquaintances he is on good terms with and never contacts. (High: E2 4, E1 12.)

Depth: substantial. His friendships are old, undemanding and maintained at low frequency — months without contact, then a three-hour conversation that resumes exactly where it stopped. He does not need maintenance rituals and does not provide them, which works with people who are similar and fails with people who are not.

What he offers: loyalty, accuracy, practical help, memory of what you said, and an absence of judgement about your life choices as long as they are coherent. What he does not offer: initiation, celebration, emotional mirroring, and presence at events he finds pointless.

How friendships end: not in conflict but in attrition. Someone moves, nobody calls, two years pass, and the friendship is technically alive and practically over. He does not experience this as a loss at the time, which is why it happens repeatedly. (Medium-high.)

7.10 Family

With family he is dutiful (C3 29) and uncommunicative (E1 12, A2 27). He attends, he helps with logistics and money, he does not call to chat, and he does not perform the emotional rituals that families use to prove closeness. Relatives who need visible affection will conclude he does not care; relatives who need someone reliable will find him the most dependable person in the family.

He is likely the one who ends up handling the practical crisis — the hospital paperwork, the estate, the difficult phone call — because he is competent and cannot refuse a duty. He is likely the last to be thanked for it.

With parents, a specific pattern is common in this profile: a late realisation, usually after a loss, that duty was not the same as intimacy, and that he gave the first and withheld the second without ever deciding to.

7.11 Romance and partnership (summary; section 14 goes deeper)

He is not a pursuer. Low E3, low E1 and high N4 mean that in the early stage he waits, misreads signals, and declines to risk an unambiguous move. Relationships tend to begin because the other person initiated, or because proximity made the decision for both of them. He is loyal once committed, physically undemonstrative, verbally honest to the point of injury, and quietly resentful of demands on his solitude. He needs a partner who reads low expression correctly and who is willing to run the social side of the shared life.

7.12 Digital life

His natural habitat. Text over voice, forums over gatherings, long-form over short-form. A large part of his social and intellectual life is probably online, where E2 and E1 cost nothing and O5 gets fed. The risks are specific: information as anaesthesia (endless reading as a way to not act), late-night scrolling on the N5/N3 axis, and the substitution of online intellectual company for the real relationships he does not build. The benefit is equally specific: online is where his written competence is visible without requiring assertiveness, which makes it a genuine professional asset.

7.13 Civic, political, religious

Sceptical, non-joining, principle-driven. High O6 with low A1 produces a person who distrusts both tradition and its fashionable replacements, who holds strong abstract positions and refuses to belong to the organisations that carry them. He probably votes, thinks about politics more than most, argues about it precisely, and has never been a member of anything. On religion, the likely pattern is either a private, unorthodox spirituality or a principled absence — either way, not a congregation. (Medium: O6 28, A1 17, E2 4.)

7.14 Crisis

This is where the profile is at its best and its most misleading. In an acute crisis — a medical emergency, a work disaster, a family collapse — he becomes calm, focused and effective. The anxiety that normally runs as background noise is useful under real threat: it scans, it prioritises, it prepares. People who have only seen his quiet everyday mode are surprised by how good he is in a crisis.

The cost arrives afterwards. Two days to two weeks of flatness, withdrawal, sleep disruption and self-criticism about how he handled it, none of which he reports. If a second crisis arrives during the recovery period, the collapse is disproportionate. (Medium-high: N6 20, E4 7.)

7.15 Humour

Dry, ironic, structurally precise, occasionally bleak. He is funny in writing and quiet in company, which means his humour reaches few people. It is observational rather than performative: he notices the absurdity in a system, a phrase or a rule and names it in one line. He does not tell jokes; he says the true thing in a way that is funny because it is true. (Medium-high: O5 28, A2 27, E1 12.)

7.16 Time

He underestimates how long things take, because he estimates for his best hours rather than for his actual ones. He has a poor felt sense of the passage of weeks — long stretches disappear into routine and he cannot say what happened in them. Deadlines feel distant until they are immediate, at which point the competent burst arrives. He is punctual for obligations to others and vague about his own schedule. (Medium: C2 9, C5 11, C3 29.)

7bis. Scenario library: twenty situations, played forward

Abstract traits are easy to agree with and hard to recognise. What follows are twenty concrete situations, each played forward from the scores, with the internal experience and the visible behaviour kept separate — because the gap between them is the most characteristic thing about this profile.

1. He is asked, in a meeting, what he thinks

Inside: he has already formed a view, and it is more complete than the one on the table. He runs a fast check: is it worth saying, will it be received, will he have to defend it. The check takes two seconds and usually fails. Outside: "I'd need to look at it more closely." Afterwards: irritation at himself, then at the meeting, then a private, well-argued demolition of the plan that he delivers to one colleague in the corridor.

2. A colleague's work is bad

Inside: he sees exactly what is wrong, in order of severity. Outside: he says the first item, plainly, without preamble, in front of whoever is present. Afterwards: genuine puzzlement at the reaction, because the statement was accurate and he did not raise his voice. If the colleague asks for help, he will spend two hours fixing it and do it well.

3. A family wedding

Inside: the arithmetic runs immediately — four hours, eighty people, no exit, tomorrow is lost. Outside: he attends, arrives on time, sits at the edge, is polite and silent, leaves before the dancing, and is described for years afterwards as "the one who doesn't like us". Afterwards: two flat days that he does not connect to the wedding.

4. He is offered a promotion with more responsibility

Inside: the first reaction is not ambition but cost — more meetings, more people, more administration, less reading. Outside: he asks for time to think, does not answer for a week, and either declines with a logical justification or accepts and quietly resents it. Risk: the decline is remembered, and no second offer arrives.

5. A message arrives that could be read as criticism

Inside: immediate arousal, a scan for the worst interpretation, three hypothetical replies written and discarded. Outside: no reply for two days, then a short, dry, entirely reasonable one. Afterwards: he rereads the original message twice more to check whether the tone was really there. This is the single most common invisible cost in his week.

6. His phone rings from an unknown number

Inside: aversion, a calculation of what it could be, a preference for any other channel. Outside: he lets it ring and texts afterwards. If the call was important, he will be annoyed with himself for a day and still not change the behaviour.

7. A project has a boring final stage (formatting, filing, submission)

Inside: the interesting part is over, and the remaining part is administratively unpleasant. Outside: the work sits unfinished for four days, then is completed in one long late-night burst, competently, an hour before the deadline, with two required attachments missing.

8. A friend is in serious emotional distress

Inside: concern, and a strong instinct to solve rather than to sit with it. Outside: "Have you considered…" followed by three concrete options, then silence while the friend cries, then a practical offer (money, a lift, a call he will make on their behalf) that is genuinely generous. Missed: the sentence the friend needed, which was "that sounds awful".

9. He is praised in front of the team

Inside: acute discomfort, a wish to correct the record, a suspicion that the praise is strategic. Outside: "It was mostly the data team." Afterwards: he cannot repeat the praise to anyone, and will not mention the achievement even to people who would be pleased by it.

10. He is asked to do something he thinks is wrong

Inside: a clear judgment, a list of reasons, no intention of complying with the part he disagrees with. Outside: he states the objection once, calmly, and then does the minimum required and no more. He does not escalate, does not go over anyone's head, does not organise resistance. Result: the thing is half-done, the manager is confused, and nobody can point to a refusal.

11. A friend recommends a new restaurant

Inside: no interest, mild resistance, a preference for the place he knows. Outside: "Maybe another time." Pattern: this is not about the restaurant; it is O4 at 28% applied to every proposal he will receive this year.

12. He has a free Saturday, entirely unscheduled

Inside: pleasure at the emptiness, then a slight unease at having no structure. Outside: reading, coffee, a long walk, music, an unfinished argument in his head, a nap. Nothing happens; nothing is produced; he is content and cannot explain to anyone why the day was good.

13. He is in a queue, or on hold, or waiting

Inside: disproportionate irritation, a sense of wasted time, a strong urge to abandon the task and lose the deposit. Outside: he stays, jaw tight, says nothing, and then writes a precise and slightly lethal complaint afterwards.

14. Someone cries in front of him

Inside: alarm, no script, a wish for the moment to end. Outside: stillness, an offer of water, a factual question, an awkward hand on the shoulder. He is not unmoved — his own emotional response is real and arrives later, alone — he simply has no procedure for the situation.

15. He has to choose between a well-paid job and an interesting one

Inside: the interesting one is obviously better, and he cannot quite say why, and he suspects that "interesting" is a luxury he is rationalising. Outside: he takes the interesting one and spends two years worrying about the money. Note: this is the one decision type where his C4 score works in his favour.

16. A relative asks, at a gathering, when he is getting married

Inside: irritation, a defensive calculation, a wish to leave. Outside: a flat factual answer that ends the conversation and offends the relative without meaning to. He does not have a light way of deflecting this and would not use it if he had.

17. He is given feedback that he is "difficult to read"

Inside: surprise — he believes his state is obvious, because it is obvious to him — followed by a private review of everything he has said in the last month. Outside: nothing. He does not ask what would help, and the feedback produces a week of self-scrutiny instead of one changed behaviour.

18. A crisis at work, ten people, no plan

Inside: unusual clarity. The background anxiety stops being noise and becomes signal; priorities arrange themselves; the social dimension is irrelevant because everyone is busy. Outside: he is the calmest person there, gives three precise instructions, solves the technical core, and is quietly excellent. Afterwards: two days flat, and nobody notices the connection.

19. He is alone at 1 a.m., phone in hand

Inside: tired, slightly low, looking for something that will settle the system. Outside: an unplanned purchase, a message he will regret, three hours of reading about something irrelevant, a decision taken in this state that will need to be undone. The rule that would help: nothing irreversible after eleven.

20. Someone asks him what he wants

Inside: a long inventory of things he would like (time, quiet, a good book, an interesting problem, enough money, no meetings) none of which sound like an answer to the question. Outside: "I don't know. Nothing much." Truth: he knows exactly, and has never assembled it into a sentence, and this is why other people's plans for him keep winning.

How to use these. Score each scenario as matches, partly matches or does not match. Scenarios 1, 5, 7, 10, 18 and 20 are the ones that most directly follow from the highest and lowest scores in the protocol; if those six do not match, the interpretation is wrong and the raw data should be re-read rather than trusted to this document.

9. Vulnerabilities and derailers

A derailer is a strength that breaks under load. Each of the following is a real risk for this profile, with an early-warning sign that shows up before the failure does.

9.1 Under-utilisation

Mechanism: invisible competence loop (Mechanism 1). Trajectory: work below his level, boredom, disengagement, a reputation for being capable but unambitious, a career that plateaus at thirty-five. Early warning: he stops mentioning work at all at home; he starts reading more during working hours; a private contempt for colleagues begins to show in dry remarks. Cost: the largest financial and existential cost in the profile.

9.2 Social atrophy

Mechanism: social battery plus narrowing corridor (Mechanisms 6 and 12). Trajectory: the friend group shrinks by attrition; the phone stops ringing because he stopped calling; loneliness becomes the background condition of his forties. Early warning: he declines two invitations in a row; a weekend passes with no conversation longer than a transaction; he begins to prefer the online version of every relationship.

9.3 The duty cage

Mechanism: Mechanism 8. Trajectory: years in a situation that has stopped working, followed by an abrupt, poorly planned exit that damages what the staying was meant to protect. Early warning: he starts describing obligations as things done "to him"; he counts years instead of describing the work; sleep and mood deteriorate without any external event.

9.4 Depressive consolidation

Mechanism: N3 plus isolation plus inactivity. Trajectory: a low-mood period that never fully lifts, in which the intellectual account of the mood ("this is just what my life is") replaces the possibility of change. Early warning: loss of interest in the things he loves — music, books, aesthetics — which for him is the most reliable sign, because those are his most stable pleasures.

9.5 The honest blow-up

Mechanism: A2 plus suppressed N2 plus A4. Trajectory: months of accurate observations withheld, then one sentence delivered with accumulated force at the wrong moment to the wrong person, causing damage disproportionate to the content. Early warning: he begins editing himself in one relationship specifically; the internal monologue gets sharper; he rehearses confrontations he does not hold.

9.6 Impulse residue

Mechanism: N5 plus N1. Trajectory: a series of decisions taken in bad hours — a resignation, a purchase, a message, a move — each individually defensible and collectively incoherent. Early warning: late-night activity; decisions taken without telling anyone; a pattern of "I just decided" explanations.

9.7 Physical neglect

Mechanism: E4 plus C5 plus N3. Trajectory: a decade of low activity, poor sleep and skipped check-ups, producing the standard metabolic cluster in the forties. Early warning: clothes fitting differently; stairs producing a comment; a medical appointment postponed twice.

9.8 Contempt

Mechanism: C1 plus O2 plus A6 plus A5. Trajectory: a quiet, well-argued superiority toward ordinary people and ordinary pleasures, which protects his self-esteem and costs him company. Early warning: he stops finding anything in popular culture interesting; he describes other people's choices as "fine, I suppose"; he is bored by everyone he meets within twenty minutes.

10. Blind spots: what he almost certainly does not see

Blind spots are not weaknesses. They are systematic errors in self-perception, predictable from the profile, and invisible from inside.

1. He thinks he is being clear; he is being cold. A2 at 84% plus E1 at 38% means his statements arrive with no emotional wrapper, and he evaluates them by content only. When someone reacts badly, he concludes the person is oversensitive, not that the delivery was harsh. The evidence he would need — a comparison of the same sentence with and without a warm clause — is exactly the experiment he never runs.

2. He thinks he is being modest; he is being invisible. A5 at 72% feels like virtue from inside. From outside, a man who never claims credit is a man who does not want it, and organisations allocate on the basis of claimed credit. He interprets the resulting lack of advancement as evidence that the system is unfair, which it is, without seeing his own contribution to it.

3. He thinks his withdrawal is communication; it is absence. Low compliance plus low assertiveness produces a pattern in which he stops cooperating and expects the other party to notice. They usually do not. He experiences their failure to notice as proof that they do not care, which is a conclusion built on an unshared premise.

4. He mistakes analysis for action. With O5 at 88%, understanding a problem produces real satisfaction, and that satisfaction is close enough to the satisfaction of solving it that the difference disappears. Years can pass in which he understands his situation with great precision and changes nothing.

5. He overestimates how much others know about him. Because his inner life is vivid and continuous, he assumes it is legible. It is not: E1, E2 and E6 are all low, and his face gives almost nothing. People are working with roughly ten per cent of the information he thinks he has transmitted.

6. He underestimates the cost of his silences. He does not consider silence a communication, so he does not count its effects. To the people around him, silence is the loudest thing he does.

7. He believes his low ambition is a values choice, and partly it is. But C4 at 31% is not a philosophy; it is a temperament. The story he tells about preferring freedom may be true and may also be a rationalisation of an absence of drive. Both readings can coexist, and he is unlikely to test which is which, because the untested version is more flattering.

11. The self-report problem: how this reading could be wrong

An honest report has to state its own failure modes. Five alternative explanations deserve consideration, and each would change the interpretation if it turned out to be true.

State versus trait. The protocol was taken on one day. If he was in a low period, N is inflated and E slightly depressed — both are state-sensitive. The facet pattern would still be informative (the shape is more stable than the level), but the absolute elevation of N should be treated as an upper bound.

Depressive realism. Some high-N, low-E patterns are accurate perceptions rather than distortions. A man who reports that people are unreliable may be reporting data. This matters because the standard advice for high N — "your fears are exaggerated" — can be false, and treating an accurate perception as an anxiety symptom is both ineffective and insulting.

Cultural display rules. In a context where warmth is expressed through hospitality and obligation rather than verbal affection, low E1 does not mean what a Western norm table implies. Some of the "coldness" in this profile may be a register, not a deficit. The bluntness is still real; the coldness may be partly a translation problem.

Job fit masquerading as personality. A civil-servant role that under-uses his ability will depress measured achievement striving and elevate measured dutifulness (because duty is the only rewarded channel). Some of C4's low score may be a rational response to a role with no upside, not a stable trait.

Self-serving bias in the "honest" facets. A2, A5 and O6 are the facets most flattering to endorse. A person who values honesty will over-report honesty. The corroboration here is that the three high scores are consistent with his low A1 and low A4 (he is not painting a saintly picture), which makes the honesty cluster more credible than it would be alone.

How to test this document. Pick the ten predictions in section 7 that are most checkable — the desk, the meeting behaviour, the phone calls, the late bills, the two-decision-mode pattern, the crisis competence and the post-crisis flatness, the friend count, the boredom-then-impulse sequence, the silence in conflict, the inability to ask for a raise. If eight or more hold, the profile reading is sound. If half hold, the scores are being read through too strong a lens and the facet-level story should be rebuilt from the raw data.

12. Work and career

12.1 The fit formula for this profile

He performs when four conditions are met simultaneously: the work has cognitive content (O5 28), the obligations are defined (C3 29), the social load is low (E2 4, E3 4), and the administration is someone else's problem (C2 9, C5 11). Remove any one and performance degrades in a specific way: remove cognitive content and he drifts and despises the job; remove defined obligation and nothing gets done until a deadline appears; raise the social load and he burns out quietly; make the administration his and things fall through the cracks.

The formula explains why his current role (civil servant, "کارمند") is simultaneously comfortable and corrosive. It probably supplies defined obligation and low social load, which suits him, and probably lacks cognitive content and offers no route to advancement, which is exactly the configuration that recycles the invisible-competence loop.

12.4 The leadership question

With E3 at 13% and A4 at 44%, formal leadership is a poor fit — not because he could not do it well in substance, but because the job is 70% social influence and he has almost no appetite or instinct for it. If he leads, it will be as a technical authority: leading by being the person whose judgment is trusted, not by occupying the chair. That works in small expert teams and fails in political organisations.

If he must manage: he will be fair, undramatic, honest and hands-off, and he will avoid the two things management actually requires — giving warm feedback and enforcing uncomfortable accountability. His feedback will be accurate and too late.

12.5 Team role

The specialist, the reviewer, the person who finds the flaw in the plan, the one who writes the document nobody else wants to write. Not the coordinator, not the presenter, not the morale officer. In a well-designed team he is the highest-value member per unit of noise. In a badly designed team he is the person whose contribution is discovered only after he leaves.

12.6 What would actually change his trajectory

Not effort. He already works hard when engaged. Four specific interventions, in order of leverage:

  1. Make the work visible in a medium he is good at. Written artefacts, documentation, a portfolio, a short internal publication. This bypasses E3 and N4 entirely: the work is seen without him presenting it.
  2. Acquire one ally who speaks in rooms he does not enter. A single extroverted colleague who knows the quality of his work and names it. This is the standard workaround for low assertiveness and it has a high success rate because his work is genuinely good.
  3. Convert knowledge into a credential or a body of work. A certification, a published piece, a course taught, a system built. Without conversion, O5 produces pleasure and no equity.
  4. Negotiate once, in writing, per year. A single written request for a specific change (salary, scope, title, flexibility) with reasons attached. Writing removes the social pressure that defeats him in conversation, and frequency is what matters, not charisma.

12.7 Realistic five-to-ten-year outlook

If nothing changes: stability, adequacy, slow erosion. He remains in the same band, becomes the reliable person others depend on, retires with a pension and a private sense of unused capacity. Not a disaster; a truncation.

If the four interventions above are applied: a move into an expert niche, probably in the same institution, at a higher grade and with more autonomy, with a smaller social load and more cognitive content. Realistic, not glamorous, and genuinely better.

If the narrow corridor is not addressed: the risk is not a career failure but a life failure — the sense, at fifty, of having been somewhere else for twenty years while the work happened.

13. Relationships

13.1 What he is like to be with

Inside a relationship he is loyal, honest, stable, undemanding and difficult to read. He does not lie, does not flirt, does not play games, and does not leave. He also does not initiate, does not celebrate, does not offer verbal affirmation, and does not notice emotional states until told.

The early months are usually good because proximity creates interaction and the other person carries the social energy. The middle years are where the mismatch shows. A partner who needs words will be starving next to a man who feels deeply and says nothing. A partner who needs space will find him ideal. The same trait is either the reason it works or the reason it fails, depending entirely on the other person.

13.2 His needs, stated plainly

  • Solitude as a requirement, not a mood. Daily, protected, non-negotiable. This is not rejection and it will not change.
  • Low social load. A relationship that requires frequent hosting, extended family events and a busy social calendar will exhaust him into withdrawal.
  • Intellectual company. The one thing he genuinely needs from a partner: someone who will talk about something real. Conversation is his primary intimacy channel.
  • Directness. He does not read hints, does not decode implication, and does not respond to indirect complaint. Stated needs get met; implied ones do not.
  • Time to decide. Pressure on a decision produces either a delay or an impulsive, regretted answer.

13.3 What a partner will experience, in sequence

Month one: attentive in an unusual way — he listens properly, remembers everything, and asks real questions. Month six: the silence begins to feel like distance; he stops explaining himself; the invitations come from one side. Year two: the partner is running the couple's social life and the couple's emotional life, and beginning to feel alone in a room with him. Year five: a stable but under-expressed arrangement, in which the partner has learned to ask precise questions and he has learned to answer them, or a rupture in which the partner says "I don't know what you feel" and he is genuinely bewildered, because he has been feeling it continuously.

13.4 His conflict pattern with a partner

He does not fight. He withdraws, states the truth once, refuses to comply, and waits. The partner escalates to get a reaction; the escalation confirms his view that the conversation is irrational; he withdraws further. This is the classic demand-withdraw cycle, and his contribution is the withdraw side. It is not coldness; it is a nervous system with a very low tolerance for emotional intensity (N6 20, E3 4) plus a strong preference for accuracy over harmony (A2 27, A4 14).

The repair he can actually perform: naming the withdrawal ("I'm going quiet because I'm overwhelmed, not because I don't care; give me an hour") and returning on a stated time. This one move is worth more than any amount of attempted warmth.

13.5 Compatibility, by profile shape

Easiest fit: a partner moderately extraverted (to carry social life), high in warmth and emotional expression (to compensate for his E1), moderately high in conscientiousness for order (to compensate for C2), tolerant of solitude, and not dependent on constant verbal affirmation. In MBTI terms, the commonly cited matches for his type are ENFJ, ENTJ, INFJ and ENTP; the substantive requirement is not the letter but the two functional needs — someone who initiates, and someone who does not read silence as rejection.

Hardest fit: a partner who needs high expressed affection and interprets reserve as withdrawal of love; a partner with high social needs and an expectation of shared social life; a partner who manages conflict by escalating volume. Also difficult: a partner with a similar profile, because two low-E, low-A4, high-A2 people produce a very quiet household with a great deal unsaid.

13.6 Marriage and the single status

At thirty-four and unmarried, the profile offers a specific explanation that is not a defect: he does not pursue, he does not perform for an audience, and he does not enjoy the social formats in which meeting happens. Add N4 (fear of judgment) and A1 (low trust), and the arithmetic of meeting someone is unfavourable without an intermediary. This is a structural problem with a structural solution — introductions, shared activities with a task, small settings — not a motivational problem that will resolve with effort.

What he should not conclude: that he does not want partnership. Nothing in the protocol says that. High A5, high C3 and moderate A3 are all compatible with committed partnership; low E1 means the expression will be poor, not that the attachment is absent.

13.7 If he has children (or becomes a parent)

Probable pattern: reliable, fair, honest, intellectually engaged, and emotionally reserved. He will teach, answer questions properly, take the child's ideas seriously (O6), and will struggle with the noise, the disorder, the social logistics and the emotional labour. He will be the parent who explains things and the parent who is not warm on demand. The risk is a child who concludes that the parent is not interested, because interest is expressed as attention to ideas rather than as affection. The single most valuable thing he could do is state his affection in words, out loud, repeatedly, even though it feels redundant to him.

14. A development plan that respects the profile

Advice for this profile has to be mechanical, because the psychological mechanisms that normally convert intention into action (C5, C4) are precisely the ones that are weak. Everything below is a rule, a schedule or an external structure — nothing depends on him feeling motivated.

14.1 The four rules

  1. Nothing irreversible within seventy-two hours of a strong feeling. (Against N5. Write the decision down with a date; act on the date.)
  2. Every commitment has a scope and an end date. (Against C3's open-ended duty and Mechanism 8's duty cage.)
  3. One new thing per month, chosen by someone else, under two hours. (Against O4, E5 and the narrowing corridor.)
  4. One sentence of affiliation attached to every critical statement. (Against the honesty tax.)

15. The equivalent MBTI type

MBTI is not derivable from NEO-PI-R by formula, and any claim of a precise mapping should be treated with suspicion. But four of the five domains correlate with the four MBTI dichotomies strongly and repeatedly in the literature — Extraversion with E/I, Openness with S/N, Agreeableness with T/F (inversely), Conscientiousness with J/P — and a facet-level reading gives a type indication with reasonable confidence, especially when the facets within a domain agree with each other. That is exactly the case here for three of four dichotomies, and the fourth is a genuine call.

15.1 Dichotomy by dichotomy

E / I — Introversion, decisively

Evidence: E domain 55/192 (28.6%), with E2 gregariousness 4, E3 assertiveness 4, E4 activity 7, E1 warmth 12, E5 excitement seeking 13, E6 positive emotions 15. Five of six facets in the bottom third, two at the floor.

Reading: This is not a borderline call. In MBTI terms, an I with a strong preference — the kind of introvert whose energy comes from solitude, whose thinking happens before speaking, and whose social performance is a learned skill rather than a native mode. Confidence: very high.

S / N — Intuition, decisively

Evidence: O domain 133/192 (69.3%), with O5 ideas 28, O6 values 28, O2 aesthetics 26, O3 feelings 23. Against O4 actions 9.

Reading: The pattern is intuition-specific in an important way: the abstraction, the conceptual appetite, the interest in principles and possibilities, the aesthetic and theoretical orientation — all N. What is missing is the S-side concreteness that would normally appear as high O4 (hands-on novelty) and high E5 (sensation seeking), both of which are low. So he is not a "tactile, experiential" open person; he is a mental, theoretical open person. That is the textbook N. Confidence: very high.

T / F — Thinking, with a caveat

Evidence for T: A6 tender-mindedness 18 (low), A3 altruism 21 (low), A1 trust 17 (low), A4 compliance 14 (low), against A2 straightforwardness 27 and A5 modesty 23. Add C1 competence 25 and C3 dutifulness 29 — the "principle over person" cluster. Judgements are made on coherence and accuracy, not on impact on feelings.

Evidence for F: O3 feelings 23 (openness to his own emotions), and the possibility that the low warmth scores reflect inexpressiveness rather than low feeling.

Reading: The F-side evidence is real but weak, and it is about internal emotional richness rather than about decision criteria. MBTI's T/F is about how decisions are made, and his decisions are made on merit, logic and obligation — the F criteria appear only after the decision, as private feeling. The caveat is that his T is not the harsh, competitive, "objectivity as armour" T; it is a principled, modest, unsentimental T. Confidence: high.

J / P — Perceiving, with more ambiguity than the rest

Evidence for P: C2 order 9 (28%), C5 self-discipline 11 (34%), C4 achievement striving 10 (31%), N5 impulsiveness 20, O5 and O6 at 88% (openness and perceiving travel together), low need to close decisions early, discomfort with rigid plans.

Evidence for J: C3 dutifulness 29 (91%), C1 competence 25 (78%), C6 deliberation 19 (59%), and the fact that he does finish what he has committed to, on time, when someone else is waiting.

Reading: This is the one genuine judgement call. The J-side evidence is about commitment; the P-side evidence is about structure. In MBTI terms, J is not "keeps promises" — it is "prefers a decided, ordered, closed world". His world is not ordered and not closed: he does not plan, does not systematise, does not need to have decided, and is comfortable leaving things open until they must be closed. Dutifulness is better read as a moral trait that exists in both types, and the classic J markers (order, planning, closure, achievement scheduling) are all at the bottom of his profile. Confidence: moderate-high (roughly 70/30 for P).

15.2 The result: INTP

Equivalent type: INTP (Introverted – Intuitive – Thinking – Perceiving), the "Logician" in common parlance. Functional stack: Ti – Ne – Si – Fe. Estimated fit: strong on I, N and T; the P is the only soft edge, with INTJ as the nearest alternative.

15.3 Why the function stack fits him better than the four letters

Ti (dominant, introverted thinking). An internal logical framework, built and maintained privately, against which claims are tested. Signs in his data: A2 27 (says what the framework concludes), O6 28 (will not accept a proposition because it is traditional), A1 17 (will not accept it because it is popular), C1 25 (confidence in his own reasoning), E3 4 (does not externalise the framework as leadership or argument in the room). Ti dominants are precise, blunt, internally consistent, slow to act, and uninterested in persuading people who have not done the reasoning themselves. That is this profile almost exactly.

Ne (auxiliary, extraverted intuition). Idea generation, pattern-finding, possibility, cross-domain connection. Signs: O5 28, O6 28, O2 26, O1 19, plus the wide unsystematic reading life and the pleasure taken in argument for its own sake. Ne is also the function that generates twelve possible explanations for a single event and then cannot choose — which is visible in his decision pattern (C6 19 with N5 20).

Si (tertiary, introverted sensing). Memory, routine, detail, continuity, physical habit. Here the profile is informative in a negative direction: C2 order 9 and O4 actions 9 suggest an under-developed Si — no stable systems, no reliable routine, no attachment to procedure, poor administrative memory, food and route sameness that comes from inertia rather than from Si's love of the familiar. This is the classic INTP weakness, and his scores show it at the floor.

Fe (inferior, extraverted feeling). Social harmony, expressed warmth, reading the room, the desire to be liked and to belong. Signs: E1 12, E2 4, E6 15, N4 23 (fear of judgment is the classic inferior-Fe signature), A4 14 (will not smooth), A6 18 (not moved by collective sentiment). The inferior function in INTPs typically shows up as (a) awkwardness and self-consciousness in groups, (b) sudden, disproportionate concern about whether people like him, usually expressed as withdrawal rather than approach, and (c) an inability to perform the emotional rituals that hold groups together. All three are in this protocol.

The stack also explains the tension that the four letters alone do not: Ti wants correctness, Fe wants harmony, and Fe is the weakest function in the system. So he will choose correctness, and then feel the social cost as anxiety.

15.6 Other models, briefly

Enneagram. The closest fit is Type 5 (the Investigator): hoarding of energy and information, withdrawal as a default, observation instead of participation, competence as identity, a private inner world, and stinginess with time and presence (E2 4, E4 7, C1 25, O5 28). The wing is probably 5w4 rather than 5w6 — the aesthetic sensitivity (O2 26), the emotional depth (O3 23) and the unconventional values (O6 28) point to the more individualistic, feeling-adjacent wing, while the 6-wing's loyalty-to-group and anxiety-about-security pattern is weaker. The instinctual variant is likely self-preservation: resource guarding, minimal social investment, comfort in a small protected space. Possible tritype: 5-4-9, with the 9 explaining the conflict-avoidance and the withdrawal into stillness.

DISC. High C (conscientious, precise, analytical), low I (no enthusiasm display), low D (no dominance), moderate-low S (not a steady social supporter; more of a solitary specialist). The "C" profile with an unusually low interpersonal footprint.

Big Five itself is the better model here. MBTI is a coarse four-bit compression of a thirty-facet profile, and compressing loses exactly what makes this person readable: the A-domain split (honest but not warm), the C-domain split (dutiful but disorderly), and the O-domain split (open-minded but habit-bound). The type label is a useful handle; the facet profile is the object.

15.7 What the type label must not be used for

It must not be used to explain away failure ("I'm an INTP, I can't do admin"), to select a partner by letter, to predict job performance, or to excuse coldness. Type is a description of preferences, not a licence. The honest use of "INTP" here is as shorthand for the pattern already demonstrated in the scores — and the useful part of the label is not the four letters but the four functions, because they name the actual machinery: a dominant internal logic, an auxiliary idea-generator, an under-developed routine system, and a weak, anxious social function.

16. The portrait, in one piece

He is thirty-four, single, a civil servant with a master's degree, and he took a 240-item personality inventory on an autumn day in 1404. What the inventory says about him is more coherent than most profiles, and it says something specific: he is a man built for interior work in an exterior world.

The first thing anyone notices is the quiet. Not shyness exactly — the shyness is there, in the self-consciousness that makes him take the corner seat and rehearse phone calls — but underneath it is a genuine absence of appetite for the social marketplace. He does not want the room. He does not want the crowd, the noise, the small talk, the introductions, the networking, the visibility. What he wants is a chair, a light, a book that is too difficult, and four uninterrupted hours.

The second thing, which takes longer to notice, is that the quiet is not empty. Behind the flat face is one of the busiest interiors in any room he is in: a wide reading life, strong aesthetic judgments, an argument about everything, a memory for what people actually said, a private running commentary that is often very funny and almost never published. He feels a great deal — more than he shows, more than he admits — and he has almost no mechanism for transmitting it. That gap between the interior and the exterior is the central fact of his psychology, and almost every difficulty in this document descends from it.

Third: he is honest in a way that costs him. He does not flatter, does not inflate himself, does not pretend to agree, does not tell people what they want to hear. He will tell you your work is weak and then help you fix it. He will tell you his own work is weak before you notice. This is not a strategy; it is a constraint. And because it arrives without warmth, without softening, and occasionally with a flash of irritation, it is received as coldness by people who needed kindness and as integrity by people who needed the truth. Both readings are correct.

Fourth: he is dutiful in a way that traps him. If he has said he will do something, he will do it — at cost, on time, without supervision, without recognition. This is the highest score in his profile (91%) and it is genuinely admirable. It is also the mechanism by which he stays too long in jobs, cities and arrangements that have stopped working, because leaving would be a failure of an obligation and obligation outranks wellbeing in his private hierarchy.

Fifth, and this is where the profile becomes painful: he is competent and cannot show it. He knows he can do the work — competence is his second-highest conscientiousness facet — and his assertiveness is at the floor of the scale. He does not speak in the meeting. He does not ask for the raise. He does not correct the misattribution. He does not apply. Over years this produces the single most likely outcome of his life: a man whose ability and whose record have drifted apart, doing work below his level in an institution that never quite noticed him, quietly contemptuous of a system that rewards display, and privately certain that he could have done something substantial if conditions had been different.

Sixth: he worries. Continuously, privately, and with unusual intelligence. The worry is not dramatic; it is a background process that selects objects — the meeting, the symptom, the money, the email, the parents. It keeps him up. It makes him prepare. It makes him see the flaw in the plan three days before anyone else. And on bad weeks it joins with the low-mood facet and the isolation and the inactivity, and produces the flatness that he understands better than anyone around him and does nothing about, because understanding it feels like having addressed it.

Seventh: he cannot keep a system. His desk is a layer cake, his bills are late, his files are named "final2", and his best ideas live in notebooks he cannot find. This is not laziness — his dutifulness is at 91% — it is a genuine deficit in the ordering function that coexists with an unusually strong sense of obligation. The result is the most recognisable thing about him professionally: the person who delivers excellent work late and has not answered the three emails that would have made it easy to receive.

Eighth: he does not push. There is no ambition engine in the profile, and this is the fact that most determines the shape of his life. He does not want a bigger title, does not benchmark himself against peers, does not chase the salary, does not want the spotlight. He would trade a promotion for a free afternoon without hesitation. Some of this is a values position and some of it is an absence of drive, and the honest reading is that both are true at once — which is why the question "am I free or am I stuck?" will not resolve for him by introspection. It will resolve only by trying one thing and seeing what happens.

Ninth: he is narrowing. His openness to ideas and beauty is at the top of the scale; his openness to doing anything new is at the bottom. He is the man who reads three books about a country and never goes, who argues for radical change and eats the same dinner, who is unconventional in his opinions and conservative in his calendar. Nothing dramatic will happen to him. There will simply be a life that is a little smaller each year, and a rich interior that nobody is admitted to.

Tenth, and this is the counterweight to all of it: he is not broken. There is no pathology here. There is a very particular, very old human type — the scholar-clerk, the quiet sceptic, the man who keeps the records and sees the flaw and tells the truth and goes home early — and the modern world is not built for him, which is a fact about the world, not about him. He is capable of deep work, real loyalty, accurate judgment, genuine aesthetic pleasure, and unusual integrity under no supervision. Those are rare. The work of his life is not to become someone else; it is to build a structure around this person in which his competence becomes visible, his solitude is protected, his duty has an end date, and one other human being is admitted to the interior.

The MBTI equivalent is INTP — dominant introverted thinking, auxiliary extraverted intuition, under-developed routine, weak and anxious social feeling — with INTJ as the nearest alternative if one weights dutifulness and competence above disorder and openness. The Enneagram fit is 5w4, self-preservation. But the labels are only handles. The substance is in the facets: 91% duty and 28% order, 88% ideas and 28% action, 84% candour and 38% warmth, 13% assertiveness and 72% self-consciousness. Put those eight numbers together and you do not need a type at all. You have a person.

Appendix B — Limitations of this study

  • One protocol, one occasion. No retest, no informant ratings, no interview. Test-retest stability for facets is good but not perfect, and state effects are real.
  • Self-report only. Everything here is his self-view filtered through 240 items. Behavioural confirmation (section 7) is the only available check, and it is unperformed.
  • Norm ambiguity. The sheet's two keys disagree on A and C, and several facet labels look coarse. The analysis therefore relies on raw scores and within-profile rank, not on the printed labels.
  • No clinical evaluation. High N3 and high N1 raise a reasonable question about depressive and anxiety symptoms, which a personality inventory cannot answer. If low mood persists for more than two weeks with functional impairment, that is a clinical question, not a typological one.
  • No data on the second sheet beyond the chart. The T-score profile was read visually (±3); it was not used to derive any conclusion that the raw scores do not already support.
  • MBTI mapping is inferential. The correlations between the Five-Factor Model and MBTI are substantial but imperfect; the INTP conclusion is a weighted judgment, not a measurement, and the J/P edge is the weakest link.
  • Cultural context. Persian-language norms, a civil-service career and a family culture that may express warmth through obligation rather than words can all shift the meaning of the low-warmth and low-compliance scores. Where that is likely, it is flagged in section 11.
